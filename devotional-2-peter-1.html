1 and 2 Peter: Living Hope · Week 6 Devotional

Everything for Life and Godliness

God already gave you everything.

2 Peter 1:1 to 21

We turn the page from 1 Peter to 2 Peter this week, and the danger changes. In the first letter the threat came from outside: fire, suffering, a hostile world. In this one it comes from inside: smooth voices twisting grace into permission to sin. Peter's an old man now, near the end, and he's writing like a father who wants his children strong enough to stand after he's gone.

He opens with the best news he has before he names a single danger. His divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness. Read that and let it settle. Everything you need to live for God, you've already been given. Not most of it. All of it.

Here's the week. Five days on one chapter: a gift that's already been granted, a nature you get to share, a staircase you climb with everything you've got, a road home with the doors thrown open, and a lamp that won't lead you wrong. Christian life isn't getting more from God. It's growing into what He's already given.

01

Already Granted

His divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness, through the knowledge of him who called us to his own glory and excellence, by which he has granted to us his precious and very great promises, so that through them you may become partakers of the divine nature, having escaped from the corruption that is in the world because of sinful desire.

2 Peter 1:1 to 4 · ESV

Peter starts with a word he says twice: granted. It's already done. His divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness. Underline all things. Whatever it takes to live a godly life, God supplied it by His own power. You're not under resourced, and you're not waiting on a shipment that hasn't arrived. The power that raised Jesus from the dead has already handed you everything the godly life requires.

How did it come to you? Through the knowledge of Him. Not through a secret the false teachers were selling; through knowing the One who called you. The closer you know Jesus, the more you realize you already have what you were looking for.

Peter set this up in his very first sentence, so go back to verse 1. He writes to those who have obtained a faith of equal standing with ours. An apostle, an eyewitness, a man who walked three years beside Jesus looks at scattered believers who never saw Him and says your faith holds the same rank as mine. There are no second class citizens in this kingdom.

The same sentence says that faith came by the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ. One Person. The old fisherman opens his last letter by calling the Man he ate breakfast with God. Everything else in this chapter stands on that.

Most of us aren't stalled because heaven has withheld something. We're stalled because we never opened what was handed to us.

The Greek is isotimos (ἰσότιμος): of equal honor; of equal standing and privilege. It's a citizenship word, and it appears nowhere else in the whole New Testament. Peter had every reason to rank himself above the scattered believers he was writing to: apostle, eyewitness, one of the three on the mountain. He picked the one word in the language that says there's no ranking here. If you've ever felt like the believers with the better story, the longer résumé or the louder gift got a better faith than you did, isotimos is God's answer. You didn't receive a smaller portion. You received the same one.

You're not waiting on a shipment. You're standing on a supply.

Reflect

Where have you been living as if you're missing something God has already given?

Who have you quietly ranked above yourself in the faith, and what does isotimos say to that?

What would it look like this week to open what's already been handed to you instead of asking for more?

02

Partakers of the Divine Nature

By which he has granted to us his precious and very great promises, so that through them you may become partakers of the divine nature, having escaped from the corruption that is in the world because of sinful desire.

2 Peter 1:4 · ESV

Here's the staggering part. Through His precious and very great promises, you may become partakers of the divine nature. That doesn't mean you become God. It means the very life and character of God takes root in you and grows: His holiness, His love, His life. You've been pulled out of a world that's rotting from the inside because it's ruled by sinful desire, and you've been planted in the life of God Himself.

Hear the reversal. In the garden the serpent held out a promise: eat this, and you will be like God. It was a lie in the sense that mattered, because it told Adam and Eve to grab by rebellion what could only ever come as a gift. Now look at Peter. The thing they tried to steal, God freely grants in Christ. The serpent said seize it. God says receive it. That's the difference between the oldest lie and the gospel.

Don't rush past the word Peter hangs on those promises: precious. Nobody in the New Testament says precious the way Peter does. A faith more precious than gold. The precious blood of Christ. A cornerstone chosen and precious. Now precious and very great promises. This is a man who spent half his life pricing fish, and then he met Jesus and repriced the whole world. When an old fisherman calls something precious, he's weighed it. Trust his scales.

The Greek is aretē (ἀρετή): excellence; moral goodness, the strength of character that shows up in a life. Peter uses it of God Himself: He called you to His own glory and aretē. Then he turns around and asks you to add aretē to your faith. The excellence you're called to grow is a reflection of the excellence of the God who called you. You're not manufacturing a goodness you've never seen. You're growing the character of the God whose nature you now share.

The serpent said seize it. God says receive it.

Reflect

Where have you been trying to grab by effort what God has already offered as a gift?

What would change if you really believed you share in the life and character of God, not just the rules of God?

What in your life has Peter's scale repriced since you met Jesus?

03

Supply It Lavishly

For this very reason, make every effort to supplement your faith with virtue, and virtue with knowledge, and knowledge with self-control, and self-control with steadfastness, and steadfastness with godliness, and godliness with brotherly affection, and brotherly affection with love.

2 Peter 1:5 to 7 · ESV

Watch the balance Peter strikes, because it keeps you out of two ditches. He just said God has granted you everything. The very next words are for this very reason, make every effort. Grace isn't the enemy of effort. Grace is the reason for it. You don't work to get what you lack; you work out what God has already put in.

Then he gives you a staircase. Start with faith and supplement it: virtue, knowledge, self-control, steadfastness, godliness, brotherly affection, and at the top, love. It's a chain. Each quality grows out of the one before it and reaches for the next. It isn't a checklist you finish once; it's a life of steady climbing, always adding, always increasing.

This is the same balance Paul strikes with the Philippians: work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, and then in the very next breath, it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure. Read the order. You work, because God is working. Your effort isn't what makes God move. It's the evidence that He already is.

Don't add to your faith with an eyedropper. God didn't give you the divine nature in a trickle, and you shouldn't grow it in one.

The Greek is epichorēgeō (ἐπιχορηγέω): to supply generously; to furnish lavishly. It comes from the wealthy patron who funded an entire chorus for a festival out of his own pocket and spared no expense. Peter says epichorēgeō your faith with virtue and knowledge and the rest: supply it generously, not stingily. Hold onto that word, because he picks it up again in verse 11. It's the same lavish supply God pours into you, and you pour it into growth.

Grace gives, and then grace grows.

Reflect

Which quality on Peter's staircase are you making the least effort in right now, and why?

Where have you been treating effort as the enemy of grace, or grace as an excuse for no effort?

What would lavish, not eyedropper, growth look like in that one area this week?

04

Sure, Settled, and Heading Home

For if these qualities are yours and are increasing, they keep you from being ineffective or unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ. For whoever lacks these qualities is so nearsighted that he is blind, having forgotten that he was cleansed from his former sins. Therefore, brothers, be all the more diligent to confirm your calling and election, for if you practice these qualities you will never fall. For in this way there will be richly provided for you an entrance into the eternal kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

2 Peter 1:8 to 11, 13 to 15 · ESV

Peter promises what a growing life produces: you won't be ineffective or unfruitful. Then he warns what a stalled one produces. A believer who stops growing grows nearsighted, so nearsighted he's blind, and the thing he's gone blind to is the cross. He's forgotten that he was cleansed from his former sins. Stopping isn't neutral. It's forgetting what God did for you.

So be all the more diligent to confirm your calling. Not to earn it; to make it sure and settled and unmistakable, in you and to everyone watching. A growing believer is a sure believer, and a sure believer doesn't stumble into the lie.

Now look at how Peter closes the staircase. There will be richly provided for you an entrance into the eternal kingdom. Richly provided is the same word he used for your climb. You lavishly supply your faith, and at the end God lavishly supplies your welcome home. The Patron who funded your growth funds your arrival. You won't slip into the kingdom through a side door, worn out and barely qualified; the God who poured into your climb will throw the doors open.

Jump ahead to verses 13 to 15, the most tender discovery in the chapter. Peter says the putting off of his body will be soon, as our Lord Jesus Christ made clear to him, and he'll make every effort so that after his departure you may recall these things. He calls his body a tent and his death a departure. Peter doesn't face death with a philosophy. He faces it with vocabulary he picked up at the Transfiguration. Death stopped being a wall the night he saw the glory. It became a road out.

The Greek is exodos (ἔξοδος): a way out; a departure, a road out. It's the word the ESV renders departure in verse 15, and it's the word Luke uses when Moses and Elijah stood with Jesus on the mountain and spoke of his departure, which he was about to accomplish at Jerusalem. Same word. Peter stood there and heard death called an exodos by the only Man who could walk out the other side of one. Now, an old man with the executioner close, he reaches for the word he learned that night for his own dying. The exodus God led His people through, Jesus led through the grave. Your departure follows His.

Death stopped being a wall. It became a road out.

Reflect

Where have you been coasting, and what have you forgotten that God already did for you?

What would it mean to make your calling sure this week, for your own sake and for those who watch you?

How does calling death a departure, a road out, change how you hold your own fears about the end?

05

Stand On a Word That Is True

And we have the prophetic word more fully confirmed, to which you will do well to pay attention as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts, knowing this first of all, that no prophecy of Scripture comes from someone's own interpretation. For no prophecy was ever produced by the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit.

2 Peter 1:16 to 21 · ESV

Peter knows the false teachers will call his message a fairy tale. He answers with two rocks that won't move. First, the eyewitness: we did not follow cleverly devised myths. We were with him on the holy mountain. Peter saw Jesus shining with the glory of God and heard the Father say, this is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased. He isn't passing on a rumor. He saw the majesty with his own eyes.

Second, the written word: we have the prophetic word more fully confirmed. The mountain didn't replace the Book. It confirmed it. Peter plants your feet on Scripture as ground every bit as sure as what his own eyes saw, because a memory can be doubted and God's word can't.

Then he tells you what to do with it. Pay attention to it as to a lamp shining in a dark place. The world is a dark place, full of confusing voices and clever lies. You don't argue with the darkness. You light the lamp and keep it burning until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts.

He names that star, and the last chapter of the Bible tells you who it is. Jesus says it Himself: I am the root and the descendant of David, the bright morning star. The lamp is for the night. The star ends the night. You read the lamp until the Light Himself comes up over the horizon of your heart, and then you won't need the lamp to see Him, because the night will be over.

Where did the word come from? No prophecy was ever produced by the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit. The human authors were real, and they didn't steer. The Spirit carried them, the way wind fills a sail. When you open this Book you're not reading the opinions of men. You're hearing the voice of God, carried to you by His Spirit.

The Greek is phōsphoros (φωσφόρος): light bearer; the dawn star, the one who brings the day. It appears only here in the entire New Testament. The lamp is for the night; the phōsphoros ends the night. Peter hands you a lamp for the dark and points past it to the Star who's coming.

The Greek is pherō (φέρω): to carry, to bear, to be borne along. It's the word Peter uses for the Father's voice borne from heaven on the mountain, and the same word, in the form pheromenoi, for the prophets carried along by the Holy Spirit. The men who wrote it didn't invent it. They were carried, and the same Spirit who carried them still speaks through it. Trust the Book in your lap. It's a lamp you can walk by in the dark.

You don't argue with the darkness. You light the lamp.

Reflect

How are you paying attention to the Word right now: casually, or as to a lamp in a dark place?

Where do you need the lamp to guide you this week?

What's one clever voice you've been listening to that the lamp would expose?

A Prayer of Identity

Father, thank You that You've already granted me everything for life and godliness. I'm not waiting on a shipment; I'm standing on a supply. Thank You for the faith of equal standing You gave me, and for the nature You're growing in me, received as a gift and never seized. Because of what You've done, I make every effort: I supplement my faith with virtue, knowledge, self-control, steadfastness, godliness, brotherly affection and love, lavishly and not stingily. Keep me from going nearsighted. Remind me that I was cleansed from my former sins by the blood of Jesus, who died and rose again. Make my calling sure, and one day throw the doors open on my welcome home. Until the morning star rises in my heart, let Your Word be the lamp under my feet. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Declaration

His divine power has granted me everything for life and godliness.
I share the very nature of God, received as a gift and never seized.
My faith holds the same rank as an apostle's.
Because God has given me everything, I make every effort to grow.
I stand on a Word that is true, a lamp shining in a dark place.
I walk with victory, not to it, in Jesus' name. Amen!

Now go, and grow into what God has already given. May you live from His divine power, which has granted you everything for life and godliness. May you make every effort, adding to your faith until love crowns it all. May you walk by the lamp of His Word until the morning star rises in your heart.

Now to him who is able to do far more abundantly than all that we ask or think, according to the power at work within us, to him be glory in the church and in Christ Jesus throughout all generations, forever and ever. Amen. (Ephesians 3:20 to 21)
