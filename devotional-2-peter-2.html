1 and 2 Peter: Living Hope · Week 7 Devotional

The Warning Against False Teachers

It promises freedom and hands you a chain.

2 Peter 2:1 to 22

Last week Peter grounded you in everything God has already given. This week he tells you why he started there. A lie is coming, and it won't walk in wearing a warning label. It'll slip in quietly, sounding reasonable, sounding generous, sounding like freedom.

This is one of the sharpest chapters in the New Testament, and it's here for love, not fear. A shepherd who never warns the flock about wolves isn't being kind; he's being negligent. Peter loves this church too much to leave it undefended, so he names the danger plainly. And right in the middle of the warning, he plants the hope: the Lord knows how to rescue the godly and to judge the false.

Here's the week. Five days in one chapter: a wolf that doesn't knock, a test that looks past the charisma, a Judge no platform can intimidate, a worn down man the Lord reaches in and pulls out, and a counterfeit freedom that turns out to be a chain. You won't leave this week anxious. You'll leave discerning, and planted a little deeper in the truth.

01

They Deny the Master Who Bought Them

But false prophets also arose among the people, just as there will be false teachers among you, who will secretly bring in destructive heresies, even denying the Master who bought them, bringing upon themselves swift destruction. And many will follow their sensuality, and because of them the way of truth will be blasphemed. And in their greed they will exploit you with false words. Their condemnation from long ago is not idle, and their destruction is not asleep.

2 Peter 2:1 to 3 · ESV

Peter doesn't say false teachers might come. He says they will, and he tells you how they get in: secretly. The wolf doesn't stand up and announce, I'm here to lead you away from Christ. He dresses like a shepherd, uses the same words, quotes the same Book, and slowly bends it. You're rarely taken in by an obvious lie. You're taken in by a familiar voice saying something just a little off.

Notice what Peter calls destructive. Not unhelpful, not harmless. These are ideas that tear down faith and wreck lives. And the tragedy at the center is the denial. They won't deny the Master with a flat statement. They'll deny Him with a redefinition: a Jesus who asks nothing, a grace that changes nothing, a cross that costs nothing. When a teaching leaves you with a Christ who makes no claim on your life, you're hearing a denial dressed up as devotion.

Christians have read the phrase the Master who bought them in more than one way, and this devotional isn't going to settle that old debate. What isn't in dispute is the sin. These teachers stand on ground the blood of Christ purchased and refuse the One who paid for it. Peter already told you the price: not silver or gold, but the precious blood of Christ. That's what makes the denial so ugly. It isn't a stranger being rejected. It's the Owner.

Hear the opposite warning too. A false teacher isn't someone who disagrees with you about a secondary question, and the test isn't whether a teacher is polished, or loud, or new to you. The test is what the teaching does with Jesus.

The Greek is despotēs (δεσπότης) and agorazō (ἀγοράζω): master, owner of the house; to buy at the marketplace. Peter doesn't use the usual word for Lord. Despotēs is the owner of the household, the one who holds the papers and has every right of possession. Agorazō is a slave market word, to purchase at the marketplace. Put them together and verse 1 pictures runaway slaves denying the Owner who paid for them. Whatever the false teacher's brand, the question underneath is always the same: who owns you? The gospel's answer is that Jesus does, and He paid with His blood.

You're rarely taken in by an obvious lie. You're taken in by a familiar voice saying something just a little off.

Reflect

Where have you quietly accepted a version of Jesus who makes no claim on your life?

What's the difference between a teaching you disagree with and one that changes who Jesus is?

Who gets to speak into your spiritual life right now, and how do you know what they do with Christ?

02

Follow the Money and the Sacrifice

And in their greed they will exploit you with false words. Their condemnation from long ago is not idle, and their destruction is not asleep.

2 Peter 2:1 to 3 · ESV

Peter doesn't leave you guessing about the engines that drive a false teacher. He names two: greed and sensuality. They make merchandise of the sheep, and they make peace with sin instead of putting it to death. Watch for those two marks in any teaching, no matter how polished the delivery. Does it end with somebody getting rich off the flock? Does it excuse the flesh?

Here's a plain test you can carry for the rest of your life. Real shepherds spend themselves for the flock. False ones spend the flock for themselves. Watch where the money flows and where the sacrifice lands.

Later in the chapter, Peter names the patron saint of every prophet for profit: Balaam, who loved gain from wrongdoing (2 Peter 2:15). A man with a real gift who put a price tag on it, until God opened a donkey's mouth to slow him down. Think about that. The animal saw what the prophet wouldn't. When money drives the message, a donkey has more spiritual sight than the man in the pulpit. A gift isn't the proof. The love underneath it is.

None of this is new. Jeremiah stood in the temple courts while prophets promised peace, peace, when there was no peace, and God said, I did not send them, yet they ran. Ezekiel heard God indict shepherds who fed themselves instead of the flock. Deuteronomy told Israel how to test a prophet, because there would always be prophets to test. Peter's raising an old alarm for a new generation, and God has never once been fooled by a confident voice carrying a false word.

The Greek is emporeuomai (ἐμπορεύομαι) and hairesis (αἵρεσις): to make merchandise of; a chosen opinion, a faction. The word Peter uses for exploit gives us our word emporium: to treat people as merchandise, the sheep as a cash crop. And the word for the destructive ideas, hairesis, comes from the root that means to choose for yourself. It's where we get heresy. At its root it's a teaching a person picks for himself and sets above the truth God gave, and it divides the flock around a human opinion instead of uniting it around Christ. So test the teaching, not the charisma. One kind builds the body. The other splits it.

Real shepherds spend themselves for the flock. False ones spend the flock for themselves.

Reflect

When you follow the money and the sacrifice in the voices you listen to, where does each one land?

Have you ever trusted a gift or a platform more than the Word it was supposed to serve?

Which voice in your life lifts up Christ and holds people together on His Word?

03

The Judge Isn't Intimidated

For if God did not spare angels when they sinned, but cast them into hell and committed them to chains of gloomy darkness to be kept until the judgment; if he did not spare the ancient world, but preserved Noah, a herald of righteousness, with seven others, when he brought a flood upon the world of the ungodly; if by turning the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah to ashes he condemned them to extinction, making them an example of what is going to happen to the ungodly.

2 Peter 2:4 to 6 · ESV

In the verses that follow his warning, Peter piles up examples from history, and every one makes the same point. God has always known how to tell the false from the faithful, and He has always acted on it. He judged rebellious angels. He sent the flood on an ungodly world and carried Noah through it. He turned Sodom and Gomorrah to ashes. Each time, two things happened at once: the unrighteous were held for judgment, and the righteous were rescued.

That should settle something in your heart. If God didn't spare angels, then no platform, no following and no eloquence will shelter a false teacher. There's nothing in existence impressive enough to intimidate the Judge. The spiritual powers your neighbors fear are already in chains, awaiting sentence.

Don't read the pile of examples as cruelty, either. It's one God. His judgment and His rescue have always arrived on the same day, in the same story. Noah is one preacher of righteousness in a world gone entirely wrong, and the same flood that ended the old world floated him. The Judge isn't a mood swing away from being the Savior. He's both at once, and that's why you can trust Him with the verdict.

Which means you don't have to carry the verdict. You may be burdened by teachers you can't correct and movements you can't stop. Peter's answer isn't to fix them. It's to remember who holds the gavel.

The Greek is tartaroō (ταρταρόω): to cast into Tartarus; to hold in the deepest pit of restraint. This is the only place in the entire Bible this word appears. Tartarus was the deepest pit in the Greek imagination, the prison beneath every prison, and Peter reaches for his readers' own word so the point lands in their own world. Angels who sinned aren't roaming free beyond God's reach. They're in chains awaiting sentence. If that's true of angels, it's true of anyone who shapes a following out of lies. Nothing and no one is bigger than the Judge.

There's nothing in existence impressive enough to intimidate the Judge.

Reflect

Where have you been intimidated by a loud voice, a big platform or a large following?

What changes if you remember that God judges and rescues in the same breath?

What are you carrying that was never yours to carry, because the verdict belongs to the Judge?

04

Righteous Lot, Worn Down

And if he rescued righteous Lot, greatly distressed by the sensual conduct of the wicked (for as that righteous man lived among them day after day, he was tormenting his righteous soul over their lawless deeds that he saw and heard); then the Lord knows how to rescue the godly from trials, and to keep the unrighteous under punishment until the day of judgment.

2 Peter 2:7 to 10 · ESV

Here's the comfort in the middle of the warning. Verse 9 is meant to steady your heart: the Lord knows how to rescue the godly from trials. Underline knows how. God isn't caught off guard by wolves in the church. He isn't wringing His hands wondering how to protect His people. He knows exactly how to pull the godly out and hold the false accountable, and He's done it in every generation. You don't have to save yourself from every clever lie by your own cleverness. Your job is to stay close to the Shepherd and planted in the truth. His job is the rescue.

Then Peter surprises you with his example. Read Lot's story in Genesis and you find a man who chose the well watered land, pitched his tent toward Sodom and then moved inside the gates. Genesis never once calls him righteous. Peter, writing by the Spirit, calls him righteous three times in two verses: righteous Lot, that righteous man, his righteous soul. Lot's righteousness isn't earned by his record. It's what God says of a man who belongs to Him.

That's somebody reading this. You're worn down by what you see and hear every day, grieved by it, and quietly wondering if the grief means you're failing. Peter says the opposite. The ache is the evidence. A soul at home in Sodom feels nothing. Yours is tormented because it belongs to God. And the God who counted a worn down man righteous, and sent angels to take him by the hand, hasn't lost track of you.

The Greek is kataponeō (καταπονέω): to wear down, to exhaust under a weight. The word behind greatly distressed pictures a person ground down by a load that doesn't lift. Lot wasn't casually bothered. He was exhausted by what he lived among. And the word Peter uses for the rescue, rhyomai (ῥύομαι), is to deliver, to drag someone out of danger to safety. It's the same word Paul uses when he says the Father has delivered us from the domain of darkness and transferred us to the kingdom of his beloved Son (Colossians 1:13). The Lord doesn't just hope to rhyomai the godly. He knows how.

The ache is the evidence. A soul at home in Sodom feels nothing.

Reflect

Where have you been carrying the weight of guarding your own soul as if it all depends on your cleverness?

What are you grieved by right now, and what does Peter's word about Lot say to that grief?

What would it look like to stay close to the Shepherd today instead of scanning for wolves?

05

Whatever Overcomes a Person

These are waterless springs and mists driven by a storm. For them the gloom of utter darkness has been reserved. For, speaking loud boasts of folly, they entice by sensual passions of the flesh those who are barely escaping from those who live in error. They promise them freedom, but they themselves are slaves of corruption. For whatever overcomes a person, to that he is enslaved.

2 Peter 2:17 to 22 · ESV

Peter reaches for two pictures, and both promise something and deliver nothing. A waterless spring: you're dying of thirst, you run to the well and it's dry. And mists driven by a storm: clouds that look like rain on parched ground and blow past without a drop. That's false teaching. It advertises life and leaves you empty.

Then he exposes the cruelest lie of all. They promise freedom, but they themselves are slaves of corruption. This is the con at the heart of every false gospel, and it's as old as Eden. The serpent's whole pitch was freedom: God's holding out on you, so break the rule and finally be your own (Genesis 3:5). They took the freedom and walked out of the garden as slaves, hiding in the bushes from the God who used to walk with them. Every counterfeit since has run the same play: promise the garden, deliver the chains.

In Peter's world, everybody knew the rule of war. A soldier defeated on the field didn't go home; he became the slave of the one who beat him. Whoever defeats you owns you. That's why Jesus' words land so hard: everyone who practices sin is a slave to sin, and if the Son sets you free, you'll be free indeed (John 8:34 to 36). In Christ, you don't fight for your freedom, hoping to win it. The cross already won the field. You fight from His victory, not toward it.

Real freedom isn't the absence of a master. It's having the right Master. Sin arrives dressed as liberty and stays as a warden. Jesus arrives asking for everything and hands you your life back.

And Peter closes with two blunt proverbs, the dog returning to its own vomit and the washed sow back in the mud. A person can clean up the outside, brush against the truth and never be changed within. The answer isn't a better behavior plan. It's the new birth Peter preached in week one. God promised, I will give you a new heart (Ezekiel 36:26), and only a new nature breaks the old cycle.

The Greek is eleutheria (ἐλευθερία) and oligōs apopheugontas (ὀλίγως ἀποφεύγοντας): freedom, liberty; just now escaping. Eleutheria is a real word, and the false teachers dangle it, but the offer is a fraud: they promise eleutheria while they themselves are slaves. True eleutheria isn't doing whatever you want. It's being freed from the sin that owned you. And watch who they hunt: those oligōs apopheugontas, the ones barely escaping, believers with one foot just out of the old life. Wolves don't attack the center of the flock. They work the edges. So the newest believer is the one most targeted, and the saints who have walked longer are the guard. The Gathering doesn't leave its newest lambs alone at the edge. Get them to a table.

Real freedom is not the absence of a master. It's having the right Master.

Reflect

What was sold to you as freedom that has quietly become the thing that owns you?

Who is at the edge of your circle right now that you could walk beside this week?

Where do you need a new heart rather than another fresh start, and will you bring it to Jesus?

A Prayer of Identity

Father, thank You that the Master who bought me is the Shepherd who keeps me. Thank You that You knew exactly how to pull the godly out in every generation, and that You know how to do it for me. Teach me to test every voice by Your Word and by its fruit, and to ask first what a teaching does with Jesus. Keep me from the dry well and the rainless cloud. Take the chains off wherever a counterfeit freedom has wrapped around me, and give me the real freedom that only the Son gives. Where I've been tempted to guard my own soul by my own cleverness, remind me that the rescue is Yours. Thank You for the blood of Jesus, who died for me and rose again, and who is able to keep me from stumbling. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Declaration

I belong to the Master who bought me with His own blood.
The Lord knows how to rescue the godly, and He knows how to rescue me.
I test every voice by the Word and by the fruit, and I follow only the Shepherd.
Jesus has taken my chains off, and the Son has set me free indeed.
I don't fight for freedom. I walk with victory, not to it, in Jesus' name. Amen!

Now go, and stay planted in the truth. May you test every voice by the Word and by the fruit, and follow only the Shepherd. May you drink from the living fountain and never settle for a dry well. May you walk in the freedom Christ gives, freed from every master but Him.

Now to him who is able to keep you from stumbling and to present you blameless before the presence of his glory with great joy, to the only God, our Savior, through Jesus Christ our Lord, be glory, majesty, dominion, and authority, before all time and now and forever. Amen. (Jude 24 to 25)
