Malachi: The Coming Messenger · Week 2 Devotional

Broken Covenants

God is a witness to what you promised.

Malachi 2:1 to 17

Last week God opened Malachi with four words, “I have loved you,” and His people shrugged. This week He walks out of the sanctuary and into the kitchen. Same disease, new room. The priests have corrupted the covenant they were given, husbands are walking out on the wives of their youth, and a tired people are asking whether God is even watching.

Here’s the week. Five days in Malachi 2, and one picture runs through all of it: every covenant has somebody standing up front. A witness. The One who saw the vow made. Malachi 2:14 says it out loud: “the LORD was witness.” He saw Levi’s covenant cut. He saw the wedding. He saw the tears on the altar, and He saw who left and who got left.

God takes covenant seriously, at the altar and at the kitchen table, because He’s a covenant keeper, and He’s making covenant keepers. This week isn’t about trying harder to keep your word. It’s about meeting the One who kept His.

01

You Can Run a Flawless Service With an Empty Heart

“And now, O priests, this command is for you. If you will not listen, if you will not take it to heart to give honor to my name, says the LORD of hosts, then I will send the curse upon you and I will curse your blessings. Indeed, I have already cursed them, because you do not lay it to heart. Behold, I will rebuke your offspring, and spread dung on your faces, the dung of your offerings, and you shall be taken away with it.

Malachi 2:1 to 3 · ESV

God opens Malachi 2 on the worship leaders. The priests. The men who handled holy things every single week, who knew the order of service by heart and could run it in their sleep. That last part was the problem.

Look where God puts His finger. Not on the animals this time. On the heart. “If you will not take it to heart.” He says it twice in one verse, once as a warning and once as a diagnosis: “you do not lay it to heart.” Nothing on the outside had to change for the inside to leave. You can run a flawless service with a heart that left the building months ago.

God says something next that ought to stop us cold. “I will curse your blessings.” The priest’s whole job was to stand up and bless the people with the words of Numbers 6, “The LORD bless you and keep you.” God says, keep treating My name light, and I’ll turn the blessing in your mouth into a curse. Verse 3 gets graphic on purpose. The dung of the sacrifices, the waste the Law said to carry outside the camp and burn, God says He’ll spread on the faces of the men who brought Him leftovers, and haul them out with it. That’s how seriously God takes the people who lead His people.

Don’t read this as God losing His temper. Read it as God refusing to let His people go through the motions, because He loves them too much to leave them there. Hold onto that curse, though, because Malachi doesn’t get the last word on it. Paul does: “Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us.” The curse the faithless earned, Jesus wore on a cross. That’s the road home for every heart that’s drifted.

The Hebrew is sim ’al lev (שִׂים עַל־לֵב): to set upon the heart, to take to heart. “Take it to heart” in 2:2 is תָשִׂימוּ עַל־לֵב, tasimu ’al lev, literally “set it on the heart.” The second time God says it in the same verse, it’s שָׂמִים עַל־לֵב, samim ’al lev: you aren’t setting it on the heart. In Hebrew the heart, lev, isn’t only feelings. It’s the place where you think, decide and choose. God isn’t asking His priests to feel more. He’s asking them to put His name back where the decisions get made.

You can run a flawless service with a heart that left the building months ago.

Reflect

Where has your faith kept running on schedule while your heart quietly left the building?

What would it look like to set God’s name back on your heart, in the place where your decisions actually get made?

How does it change things to know the curse you earned was carried by Jesus?

02

God Doesn’t Sign Contracts. He Cuts Covenants.

So shall you know that I have sent this command to you, that my covenant with Levi may stand, says the LORD of hosts. My covenant with him was one of life and peace, and I gave them to him. It was a covenant of fear, and he feared me. He stood in awe of my name. True instruction was in his mouth, and no wrong was found on his lips. He walked with me in peace and uprightness, and he turned many from iniquity. For the lips of a priest should guard knowledge, and people should seek instruction from his mouth, for he is the messenger of the LORD of hosts.

Malachi 2:4 to 7 · ESV

After the warning, God shows them the real thing. Not a lecture about what’s wrong; a picture of what’s right. A priest who feared God, stood in awe of His name, told the truth, walked with Him in peace and turned many from iniquity. Malachi is reaching back to Moses blessing Levi in Deuteronomy 33, and to Numbers 25, where God gave Phinehas, Aaron’s grandson, “my covenant of peace” because he stood for God’s honor when nobody else would.

Here’s the word that holds this whole chapter together. Covenant. In Hebrew you don’t sign a covenant; you cut one. In Genesis 15 God walks between the pieces of the animals while Abraham sleeps. Blood, not ink. A contract says, here’s what I’ll do if you do your part. A covenant says, I’m binding My own life to this.

Notice who stood up front when Levi’s covenant was cut. God did. He gave it, and He watched the man who walked with Him. Verse 7 goes further and calls that faithful priest “the messenger of the LORD of hosts.” Hold onto that word. Messenger is the name of this book, and it’s coming back in Malachi 3 with a face and a name.

There’s a Priest who walked this covenant perfectly. True instruction was in His mouth, and no wrong was ever found on His lips. Jesus turned many from iniquity, kept Levi’s covenant without a single crack, and cut a better one in His own blood. Hebrews closes with a blessing that ties it together: “Now may the God of peace who brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, the great shepherd of the sheep, by the blood of the eternal covenant, equip you with everything good that you may do his will.” Life and peace were what Levi was promised. A risen Shepherd is how you get them.

The Hebrew is berit (בְּרִית) and shalom (שָׁלוֹם): covenant; peace, wholeness. Verse 5 reads בְּרִיתִי הָיְתָה אִתּוֹ הַחַיִּים וְהַשָּׁלוֹם, beriti hayetah itto hachayyim vehashalom: “my covenant was with him, the life and the peace.” Berit is a bond cut in blood, never a deal on paper. Shalom isn’t just quiet; it’s wholeness, nothing missing and nothing broken. The faithful priest of verse 7 is called מַלְאַךְ, mal’akh, “messenger,” the same word that gives this book its name.

God didn’t call you to a contract you can cancel. He cut a covenant, and He’s standing up front.

Reflect

Where have you treated your walk with God more like a contract than a covenant?

What would life and peace, nothing missing and nothing broken, look like in your home this week?

How does it settle you that Jesus kept the covenant perfectly, and then cut a better one in His blood for you?

03

Favor From God, Favorites With His Word

But you have turned aside from the way. You have caused many to stumble by your instruction. You have corrupted the covenant of Levi, says the LORD of hosts, and so I make you despised and abased before all the people, inasmuch as you do not keep my ways but show partiality in your instruction.”

Malachi 2:8 to 9 · ESV

Three verbs, and every one of them hurts. Turned aside. Caused many to stumble. Corrupted. The men who were supposed to guard knowledge had become the reason people tripped.

The last phrase is the one to sit with: “show partiality in your instruction.” The important people got a softer Word. The big donor got the easy sermon. Last week these same priests wanted God to show them favor over a blind goat; this week we find out they were playing favorites with His Word. God answers in kind. You despised My name, so you’ll be despised. You lifted the faces of men, so men won’t look at you.

Don’t sit back and think this is about priests four hundred years before Jesus. Peter shuts that door: “But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession.” Every believer is a priest now, and this covenant is ours. The worship team, the Table leaders, the kids team, the greeters at the door, the pulpit: every one of us who leads anybody, anywhere. God is a witness to what we promised when we said yes to serve. The load is shared, and so is the call to carry it faithfully.

Here’s the good news for every leader who’s ever softened the truth for the wrong reason. There’s a Priest who never once caused anybody to stumble. Hebrews says it plainly: “For it was indeed fitting that we should have such a high priest, holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens.” Jesus never lifted a face to win a crowd. He told the truth to the powerful and the forgotten alike, and He went to the cross for both.

The Hebrew is nasa’ panim (נָשָׂא פָּנִים): to lift the face; to show favor or partiality. “Show partiality” in 2:9 is נֹשְׂאִים פָּנִים, nos’im panim, literally “lifting faces.” Picture someone bowed low before a judge, and the judge reaching down to lift his chin: you’re accepted. In the right hands it’s grace. The same idiom sits behind 1:9, “will he show favor to any of you?” The priests wanted God to lift their faces while they lifted the faces of whoever could do something for them.

They wanted favor from God while they played favorites with His Word.

Reflect

Is there anyone whose face you lift, someone you soften the truth for because of what they could do for you?

Where do you lead someone, at home, at work or at church, and what did you promise God when you said yes?

How does Jesus, the Priest who never caused anyone to stumble, change the way you carry what you lead?

04

Only Somebody Inside Can Break It From the Inside

Have we not all one Father? Has not one God created us? Why then are we faithless to one another, profaning the covenant of our fathers? Judah has been faithless, and abomination has been committed in Israel and in Jerusalem. For Judah has profaned the sanctuary of the LORD, which he loves, and has married the daughter of a foreign god. May the LORD cut off from the tents of Jacob any descendant of the man who does this, who brings an offering to the LORD of hosts! And this second thing you do. You cover the LORD’s altar with tears, with weeping and groaning because he no longer regards the offering or accepts it with favor from your hand. But you say, “Why does he not?” Because the LORD was witness between you and the wife of your youth, to whom you have been faithless, though she is your companion and your wife by covenant. Did he not make them one, with a portion of the Spirit in their union? And what was the one God seeking? Godly offspring. So guard yourselves in your spirit, and let none of you be faithless to the wife of your youth. “For the man who does not love his wife but divorces her, says the LORD, the God of Israel, covers his garment with violence, says the LORD of hosts. So guard yourselves in your spirit, and do not be faithless.”

Malachi 2:10 to 16 · ESV

Watch how God starts. Not with the sin. With the family. “Have we not all one Father? Has not one God created us?” Identity first, always. Before He tells you what you did, He tells you who you are. You’re family, and that’s exactly why what comes next hurts so much.

Malachi picks up one word and won’t put it down. Faithless. Treacherous. Five times in seven verses. Judah married “the daughter of a foreign god,” and hear that right: the problem was never her ethnicity. Ruth was a Moabite, and she’s in the line of Jesus. The problem was the god she brought home. Paul gives the same reason when he writes, “Do not be unequally yoked with unbelievers.” What you bind your life to will bend your heart.

Verse 13 is the one that stops you. Tears all over the altar. Grown men weeping in the house of God, and God won’t accept the offering. They ask why, and He answers, “Because the LORD was witness between you and the wife of your youth.” I was standing up front. I was there the day you made her a promise. Your worship and your marriage are one subject, and you can’t split them. Jesus says the same thing: “leave your gift there before the altar and go. First be reconciled to your brother, and then come and offer your gift.” Peter tells husbands to honor their wives “so that your prayers may not be hindered.” The kitchen table and the altar are wired together.

Verse 15 is one of the hardest verses in the Hebrew Bible to translate, so stand on what’s clear. God made them one. He was seeking godly offspring, which means your faithfulness isn’t only about you; your kids are watching what you do with a promise. Verse 16 tells us what God is angry at: the man who stopped loving his wife and sent her away, leaving her with nothing. If you’re the one who got left, hear this. God was the Witness. He saw every bit of it, and the shame of it isn’t yours. He isn’t angry at you. He’s defending you.

If you’re divorced and remarried, single again, widowed or never married at all, you’re not on the outside of this. Every one of us has made promises. To God at the water when we were baptized. To this church when we stood up as partners. To a friend, to a business partner with a handshake, to our kids. God is a witness to every one of them. There’s a Husband who has never once dealt treacherously with His bride: “Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her.” Jesus was betrayed with a kiss by a man who ate at His table, and He still went to the cross for the people who betrayed Him.

The Hebrew is bagad (בָּגַד): to deal faithlessly, to betray from the inside. Bagad runs through 2:10, 11, 14, 15 and 16, five times in seven verses. Jeremiah uses it for a wife walking out on her husband: “Surely, as a treacherous wife leaves her husband, so have you been treacherous to me, O house of Israel, declares the LORD” (Jeremiah 3:20). A stranger can hurt you, but a stranger can’t bagad you. It takes a key to the house to betray the house. Verse 14 adds אֵשֶׁת בְּרִיתֶךָ, ’eshet beritekha, “your wife by covenant,” literally “the wife of your covenant.”

Only somebody inside the covenant can betray it. Only the One who cut the covenant can heal it.

Reflect

What promise are you keeping right now that’s costing you something, and where do you need God’s help to keep it?

If someone broke a promise to you, what would it mean to believe the shame of it isn’t yours?

Where do you see your worship and your relationships wired together this week?

05

The Witness Never Left His Post

You have wearied the LORD with your words. But you say, “How have we wearied him?” By saying, “Everyone who does evil is good in the sight of the LORD, and he delights in them.” Or by asking, “Where is the God of justice?”

Malachi 2:17 · ESV

Last week the priests said worship was a weariness. Now God turns it around: you’re wearying Me, with your words. Listen to what they’re saying. Evil pays. God’s fine with it. Or worse, He isn’t even here. “Where is the God of justice?”

That’s what broken covenants do to a people. Somebody breaks a promise and gets away with it, and it looks like God doesn’t care. The cheater prospers. The deal breaker gets promoted. The one who walked out seems happier than the one who stayed. Cynicism creeps in and whispers that God isn’t watching.

He’s been standing up front the whole time. They said God wasn’t in the room, and the Witness never left His post. He saw Levi’s vow. He saw the wedding. He saw the tears on the altar. He saw who left and who got left. God answers their question in the very next verse: “Behold, I send my messenger, and he will prepare the way before me” (Malachi 3:1). Israel waited four hundred years to hear the rest of that answer. Silence of prophets, never absence of God.

You don’t have to wait to know how it ends. Paul hands you a promise to stand on: “if we are faithless, he remains faithful.” You broke it. He kept it. On the night He was betrayed, Jesus lifted the cup and said, “This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood.” The curse the faithless earned, He wore on the cross, and on the third day God brought Him again from the dead. The God of justice didn’t stay away. He came, and He paid.

The Hebrew is mishpat (מִשְׁפָּט): justice, the right verdict. Their question is אַיֵּה אֱלֹהֵי הַמִּשְׁפָּט, ’ayyeh ’Elohei hammishpat, “Where is the God of justice?” Mishpat is a verdict handed down, things set right. They were asking where the courtroom went. God’s answer is a messenger, and then a Lord who “will suddenly come to his temple” (Malachi 3:1). At the cross justice and mercy met, because the Judge took the sentence Himself.

You broke it. He kept it. And the curse you earned, He wore.

Reflect

Where have you let someone else’s broken promise convince you God isn’t watching?

What changes when you hear “he remains faithful” spoken over the promises you’ve broken?

Who in your life needs to hear that the Witness is also the Savior?

A Prayer of Identity

Father, You’ve been the Witness to every promise I’ve ever made. I’ve been faithless, and You’ve stayed faithful. Thank You for Jesus, the Priest who never caused anyone to stumble and the Husband who never once betrayed His bride. Thank You for the cup of the new covenant, for the curse He wore on the cross and for the empty tomb that tells me Your covenant still holds. I bring You what I broke and what was broken against me. Take the shame that was never mine to carry. Holy Spirit, guard my spirit, set Your name on my heart, and make me a covenant keeper at Your altar and at my kitchen table. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

Declaration

I am a child of one Father, made by one God.
The LORD is a witness to every promise I make, and He helps me keep them.
I don’t lift faces. I tell the truth in love, because I serve the Priest who never caused anyone to stumble.
I guard my spirit, and I will not be faithless.
When I am faithless, He remains faithful. The covenant holds, because He holds it. I walk with victory, not to it, in Jesus’ name. Amen!

Now go, family of one Father, and keep your word this week, at the altar and at the kitchen table. The Witness is with you. He saw every promise, and He’ll help you keep it.

Now may the God of peace who brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, the great shepherd of the sheep, by the blood of the eternal covenant, equip you with everything good that you may do his will, working in us that which is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen. (Hebrews 13:20 to 21)

In the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Amen.
