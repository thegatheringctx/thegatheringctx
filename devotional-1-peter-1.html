1 and 2 Peter: Living Hope · Week 1 Devotional

Born to a Living Hope

Your hope is alive, because it is tied to a Christ who is alive.

1 Peter 1:3 to 25

We open a new letter this week, eight weeks in 1 and 2 Peter, and it lands on the one word every heart is hunting for. Hope. Not the thin kind that means maybe it will work out. Peter writes to people whose lives were genuinely hard, exiles scattered across five provinces, and he hands them a hope with a pulse.

Watch how he does it. He does not open with a command. He opens with a doxology. Blessed be God. Everything Peter is about to ask of a suffering church rests first on what God has already done. You were born again into a living hope, an inheritance is being kept for you in heaven, and your very soul is guarded by the power of God. For five days we live from that hope, not toward it.

01

Born Again to a Living Hope

Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ! According to his great mercy, he has caused us to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead.

1 Peter 1:3 · ESV

Peter says God caused us to be born again. Not improved. Born again. You did not tidy up the old life. God started a new one in you. And notice the engine of it. According to his great mercy. Not according to your performance. Not because you finally got it together. Mercy. He caused it.

Now stop on that word living. Most hope is dead on arrival. It is a wish with no power behind it, and it dies the moment life gets hard. But this hope is alive, because it is tied to a living Christ. Your hope is only ever as alive as the One it is anchored to. And He walked out of the grave. So your hope has a pulse.

Hear this on your hardest day. You are not hoping it holds. You are held. The life in you did not come from you, so it cannot be undone by you. It was born from a living Lord.

anagennaō (ἀναγεννάω) to be born again, to be begotten anew, to be given a brand new life from above. Peter uses it here in verse 3 and again in verse 23. God did not renovate the old you. He caused a new you to be born. This is not self improvement. It is resurrection life planted in a person. So stop trying to renovate a self that God has already made new. Live from the new one. You were not patched. You were born again.

Your hope is not a wish. It is alive, because it is tied to a Christ who is alive.

Reflect

Where have you been trying to renovate an old self that God has already made new?

What kind of hope have you been living on, the wishful kind or the living kind?

What would change today if you truly believed your new life came from mercy, not performance?

02

An Inheritance Nothing Can Touch

...to an inheritance that is imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, kept in heaven for you, who by God's power are being guarded through faith for a salvation ready to be revealed in the last time.

1 Peter 1:4 to 5 · ESV

Peter stacks three words on your inheritance. Imperishable. It cannot rot. Undefiled. It cannot be stained. Unfading. It cannot lose its color. Everything you own on earth fails at least one of those. Your car is rusting. Your body is aging. Your best day is already fading in the rearview mirror. This inheritance fails none of them.

And it is kept in heaven for you. The word kept means guarded, watched over, held under lock. Your inheritance is not out here where thieves and time can reach it. It is up there, in the safest place in the universe.

Then hear the part that steadies you. It is not only the inheritance that is kept. You are kept too. You are being guarded through faith by God's power. So the inheritance is guarded for you, and you are guarded for the inheritance. Two locks. Nothing gets to your future, and nothing gets to your soul, without going through the power of God first. And notice how He guards you. Through faith. He does not carry you asleep. He keeps you believing.

Your inheritance is kept for you, and you are kept for it. Two locks, and God holds both keys.

Reflect

What are you gripping on earth that is perishable, defiled, or fading, as if it were your real inheritance?

How would this week feel different if you believed nothing can reach your soul without going through God first?

Where do you need to trade an anxious grip for the peace of being kept?

03

Joy in the Fire

In this you rejoice, though now for a little while, if necessary, you have been grieved by various trials, so that the tested genuineness of your faith, more precious than gold that perishes though it is tested by fire, may be found to result in praise and glory and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ.

1 Peter 1:6 to 7 · ESV

In this you rejoice. In what? In everything he just said. The living hope. The kept inheritance. The guarded soul. That is where the joy comes from, and it holds even when the next line arrives. You have been grieved by various trials. Peter does not pretend the fire is not real. He says the grief is real. But he stamps two things on it. For a little while, and if necessary. The trial is not forever, and it is not random.

Then he tells you the purpose. When a goldsmith wants pure gold, he does not attack the gold. He heats it, and the fire drives the impurities to the surface so they can be skimmed off. The fire is not there to destroy the gold. It is there to prove it and purify it. Peter says your faith is worth more than gold, so of course it goes through fire. The fire is not God punishing you. It is God proving something real in you and burning off what was never gold to begin with.

God has been the refiner for a long time. He does not always keep His people out of the furnace. He gets in it with them. So the next time you are in the fire, do not only ask why is this happening. Ask what God is proving and purifying in you through it.

dokimion (δοκίμιον) the tested genuineness, the proof that comes through testing, the assaying of metal. It is the word for putting metal to the fire to show it is the real thing. Peter says the trial is the assay. It is not there to see if God can trust you. It is there to show you, and the watching world, that your faith is real gold and not fool's gold. The fire that would consume a fake faith only refines a real one.

God does not burn the gold. He burns off everything that was never gold, so what is left is pure.

Reflect

What furnace are you standing in right now, and have you called it punishment when it is proving?

What impurity might God be driving to the surface so it can be skimmed away?

Can you rejoice in the living hope even while the grief is still real?

04

Set Your Hope and Be Holy

Therefore, preparing your minds for action, and being sober-minded, set your hope fully on the grace that will be brought to you at the revelation of Jesus Christ. As obedient children, do not be conformed to the passions of your former ignorance, but as he who called you is holy, you also be holy in all your conduct, since it is written, 'You shall be holy, for I am holy.'

1 Peter 1:13 to 16 · ESV

Now Peter turns hope into a way of living, and it starts in your mind. Prepare your minds for action. Be clear headed. Then set your hope fully on the grace that is coming. Not partly. Fully. Half your hope on Jesus and half on your circumstances will tear you in two. Aim your whole hope at the one thing that cannot fail.

And a settled hope produces a holy life. Be holy, because the One who called you is holy. Hear this carefully. Holiness here is not a ladder you climb to reach God. It is the family resemblance of people who already belong to Him. Children look like their Father. You do not get holy to become His. You live holy because you already are His.

So the order never changes. Identity first, then conduct. You are His obedient children before you obey a single thing. That is the freedom in it. You are not performing for a place in the family. You already have the place, so now you carry the family name well.

You do not get holy to become His. You live holy because you already are His.

Reflect

Is your hope set fully on grace, or split between Jesus and your circumstances?

Do you treat holiness as a ladder to climb toward God, or as a resemblance you already carry?

What old passion from your former way of living is still trying to conform you?

05

You Know What You Cost

And if you call on him as Father who judges impartially according to each one's deeds, conduct yourselves with fear throughout the time of your exile, knowing that you were ransomed from the futile ways inherited from your forefathers, not with perishable things such as silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot.

1 Peter 1:17 to 19 · ESV

Here Peter tells you why it all matters, and it will reset your worth. You were ransomed. Peter's readers heard two things in that word, the slave market and the Exodus. You were owned by futile ways, empty patterns handed down to you, and you could not buy your own freedom. So God paid the price. And He did not pay it with perishable things like silver or gold. He paid it with the precious blood of Christ, the spotless Lamb. Heaven emptied its treasury, and the price was the blood of the Son.

Listen to the picture Peter reaches for. A lamb without blemish or spot. On the night God brought Israel out of Egypt, every household took a lamb without blemish, and the blood on the doorpost was the difference between death passing over and death coming in. Peter says that night was the rehearsal. You were not ransomed out of Egypt. You were ransomed out of the futile life handed down to you, and the price was the blood of the Lamb of God. Passover finally found its Lamb.

So hear this. You were bought at too high a price to keep living small, chained to old habits, believing you are worthless. If you ever wonder what you are worth, look at what He paid for you. Not silver. Not gold. The blood of Jesus. That is your price tag. Now live like someone who cost that much.

lytroō (λυτρόω) to ransom, to redeem, to buy someone out of slavery by paying the price of their release. It is the word for purchasing a slave and setting them free. Peter says you were ransomed, not with silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ. You are not cheap, and you are not still enslaved. God paid the highest price there is, and it bought your freedom. Live free, and live like you know what you cost.

If you ever wonder what you are worth, look at what He paid for you. The blood of Jesus.

Reflect

What futile way, handed down to you, are you still living as if you were still enslaved to it?

If the blood of Christ is your price tag, what lie about your worth needs to die this week?

Where is God calling you to stop living small and start living free?

A Prayer of Identity

Father, thank You that by Your great mercy You caused me to be born again into a living hope, because Jesus is alive. Thank You that my inheritance is imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, kept in heaven for me, and that I myself am guarded by Your power. When I walk through fire, teach me to see proving and not punishment, and to trust that You are burning off only what was never gold. Help me set my hope fully on Your grace and live holy, not to become Yours, but because I already am. And when I forget my worth, turn my eyes to the price. Not silver. Not gold. The precious blood of Christ. Let me live free, and live like I know what I cost. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Declaration

I have been born again into a living hope, because Jesus is alive.

My inheritance is kept in heaven, and I am guarded by the power of God.

When I walk through fire, my faith is proved like gold and comes out pure.

I was ransomed by the precious blood of Christ, so I know exactly what I am worth.

I do not put my hope in my circumstances. I set my hope fully on His grace.

I walk with victory, not to it, in Jesus' name. Amen.

Now go, and live from a hope that is alive. May you rest in an inheritance that is kept, and a soul that is guarded by the power of God. May you rejoice in the fire, knowing it proves the gold and does not destroy it. And may you live free and holy, because you were bought with the precious blood of Christ.

And after you have suffered a little while, the God of all grace, who has called you to his eternal glory in Christ, will himself restore, confirm, strengthen, and establish you. To him be the dominion forever and ever. Amen. (1 Peter 5:10 to 11)

In the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Amen.
