Special Messages · Week 1 Devotional

Worship: Who, Why, and How

Most people get worship backward. They treat it as the warm up before the real thing. But in heaven there is no warm up. There is a throne, and worship that never stops. This week we settle who we worship, who worships, why, and how. Then we stop watching and we bow.

John 4:23 to 24

Most people get worship backward. They treat it as the warm up before the real thing, the songs you get through so the message can start. But in heaven there is no warm up. There is a throne, and there is worship that never stops. Worship is not the opening act. Worship is the point. You were not made to be entertained by God. You were made to adore Him.

This week we settle worship down to the studs. Who we worship, and why He is worth it. Who worships, and why that is your truest name. Why we worship, on the good days and the hard ones. And how we worship, in spirit and in truth. Five days, one aim. That when the room goes quiet and the first note sounds, you are not a spectator waiting to be moved. You are a worshiper who already knows the One in front of you.

01

Who We Worship

Worthy are you, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor and power, for you created all things, and by your will they existed and were created.

Revelation 4:11 · ESV

Worship does not begin with you. It begins with Him. Before you ever ask what worship is or how to do it, you have to answer who it is for. John pulls back the curtain on the throne room of heaven, and the first thing we hear is not a request or a need. It is a verdict. Worthy.

The Greek word is axios, and it pictures a scale that hangs level, one side matching the other exactly. To call God worthy is to say His worth matches the weight of all the glory, honor, and power the elders can pour out, and then some. Worship is not us inflating God. It is us finally telling the truth about how much He weighs.

Watch what the elders do with that truth. They cast their crowns before the throne. Everything they were given, they hand back. They do not worship God because He is useful to them. They worship because He is worthy in Himself. And the song does not stay on the Creator. A few verses later it turns to the Lamb, Worthy is the Lamb who was slain. The One on the throne made you, and the Lamb bled for you, and both deserve the crown.

When God is small in front of you, worship feels like a chore you have to talk yourself into. When God is large in front of you, worship rises on its own. So before the song, before the feeling, settle who you are dealing with. He is worthy.

02

Who Worships

everyone who is called by my name, whom I created for my glory, whom I formed and made.

Isaiah 43:7 · ESV

Here is a question most people never stop to answer. Who is a worshiper? The gifted singer? The one who feels it the most? Isaiah answers with your birth certificate. You were created for His glory. Formed. Made. Worship is not a talent a few people are handed. It is the reason you exist.

The Hebrew word for formed is yatsar, to shape as a potter shapes clay. It is the same word used for God forming Adam from the dust. You were not mass produced. You were shaped, with hands, on purpose, for a purpose. And Isaiah names the purpose again a few verses later, the people whom I formed for myself that they might declare my praise. The Potter shaped the vessel to hold worship.

That changes how you see yourself. Worship is not something you add on top of your life. It is the thing your life was built to do. Before it is an activity, it is an identity. You are not someone who occasionally worships. You are a worshiper.

And do not miss what Jesus adds. The Father is seeking such people to worship him. God is not scanning the room for the polished or the impressive. He is seeking hearts that will bow low and say He is higher than me. He is seeking you. You do not have to earn the room. You were formed for it.

03

Why We Worship

And they sang a new song, saying, "Worthy are you to take the scroll and to open its seals, for you were slain, and by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation, and you have made them a kingdom and priests to our God, and they shall reign on the earth."

Revelation 5:9 to 10 · ESV

Why do we worship? Not to earn anything. Not to talk ourselves into a feeling. Heaven gives one reason and sings it in a new song. Worthy are you, for you were slain, and by your blood you ransomed people for God. The reason for worship is a Person and a price.

The Greek word for ransomed is agorazo, to buy in the marketplace. The agora was the open market where a slave could be bought and set free. John reaches for a commercial word and fills it with blood. You were not released on a technicality. You were purchased, and the price was the life of the Lamb.

Sit with the reach of that purchase. From every tribe and language and people and nation. No one is too far, too foreign, or too failed to be bought back. And notice what the ransomed become. A kingdom and priests to our God. They do not merely get out of slavery. They get a crown and an altar. That is who you are in Christ. Purchased, and made a priest.

Here is why this matters on a heavy Monday. If worship depended on your mood, it would collapse the first time life got hard. But worship stands on who He is and what He did. On your worst morning He is still worthy. The blood was already spilled and the price already paid, and that reason never moves. So do not wait to feel it first. Bring Him the truth of the cross, and let the feeling come running to catch up.

04

How We Worship

God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.

John 4:23 to 24 · ESV

A woman at a well once asked Jesus the oldest religious question. Where is the right place to worship, this mountain or Jerusalem? Jesus moved the whole conversation off the map. The hour is coming, He said, when worship will not be measured by where but by how. In spirit and in truth. Two rails, and worship runs on both.

The Greek word for worship here is proskuneo, to bow down toward a King, and it runs all through this passage. Worship is first a bowing. But there is a second word Paul adds, latreia, the service of a priest, worship offered with a whole life. One word is the bow on Sunday. The other is the service on Monday. Real worship needs both.

In truth means you worship the real God of the Word, not a softer god you invented to be easier to live with. When the truth about God is big in front of you, worship rises on its own. In spirit means your whole person is awake, stirred by the Holy Spirit, not watching from the back row. Your body is an instrument, not a decoration.

And worship was never meant to end when the music does. Paul writes, present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is your spiritual worship. A person can sing every word and worship nothing. A person can sit in silence with a surrendered heart and worship with all of heaven. Worship is not a song you sing. It is a life you lay down.

05

I Will Not Watch, I Will Bow

Through him then let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips that acknowledge his name. Do not neglect to do good and to share what you have, for such sacrifices are pleasing to God.

Hebrews 13:15 to 16 · ESV

We have seen who we worship, who worships, why, and how. Now comes the only response that fits. We stop watching and we start bowing. Hebrews was written to people tempted to go back to an old system of altars and animals. The writer tells them there is one sacrifice left to bring, and it is not an animal. It is praise. Through Jesus, offer up a continual sacrifice of praise.

The Greek word for praise here is ainesis, and in the Greek Old Testament it is the word for the todah, the thank offering a rescued person brought to the altar. Christ has fulfilled every blood sacrifice, and this is the offering that remains. Notice what it is made of, the fruit of lips that acknowledge His name. Worship is the harvest of a mouth that keeps telling the truth about God, out loud, on purpose, day after day.

Notice the word continually. Worship is not an event you attend. It is an offering you live. And a sacrifice, by its nature, costs you something. There are mornings when praise is easy and mornings when it is a knife. Good. Worship was never about a polished offering. It was about a worthy God and an open heart. And the very next line ties the altar to your ordinary week. Do good and share what you have, for such sacrifices are pleasing to God. Your kindness on Monday rises to Him like incense.

So here is where the week lands. For the one who has only ever been a spectator, today you stop watching and start bowing. For the one who is worn down with nothing impressive to bring, bring your open heart. The Father is seeking worshipers, not performers. He is seeking you. Carry this out past Sunday, not a song for an hour but a life offered continually. He is worthy of it.

Do you see yourself as a worshiper, or just as someone who attends worship?

Have you been waiting to feel like worshiping before you worship?

What part of your Monday life needs to be laid on the altar as worship this week?

Where have you been a spectator instead of a worshiper?

A Prayer of Identity

Father, You are worthy. You made all things, and You made me for Your glory. I am a worshiper before I am anything else, formed by Your hand to declare Your praise. Thank You that You are seeking me, not my performance. When my feelings run low, anchor me to the truth of who You are, and let the feeling come running to catch up. Wake my whole heart by Your Spirit. Take the bow of my Sunday and the work of my Monday and let all of it rise to You as one offering. By the blood of the Lamb I was ransomed and made a priest, so I will not watch. I will bow. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Declaration

I am a worshiper. It is who I am before it is what I do.
I worship because He is worthy, not because I feel it first.
I worship in spirit and in truth, with my whole life laid down.
Ransomed by the blood of the Lamb, I will not watch. I will bow.
I walk with victory, not to it, in Jesus' name. Amen.

Now go, and do not save worship for an hour. May the God who is worthy be large in front of you all week. May your heart bow low before your voice ever sings, and may the feeling come running to catch up. May you worship in spirit and in truth, your work and your words laid on the altar as a continual sacrifice of praise. The Father is seeking worshipers, and He has found you. Go and give Him what He is worthy of.
