Ages 4–7 · Philippians Week 3

Work It Out!

5 days with your family · Philippians 2:12–30

“For it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure.”

Philippians 2:13, ESV

Read together with your grown-up and earn +10 points each day!
1

Day 1

God Is Working Inside You!

The Bible Says

“For it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure.”

Philippians 2:13, ESV

Have you ever seen a power plant? A power plant is a big building that makes electricity. When you flip a light switch, the power is already there. It comes from somewhere else!

God is like that for us. When you do something kind or brave or right, the power to do it comes from God! He is working inside you. You do not have to make the power yourself. You just have to turn the switch by deciding to cooperate with what God is already doing.

God is not far away watching to see if you do a good job. He is inside you right now, giving you the want-to AND the can-do. That is really good news!

Let's Talk About It

  • Can you think of something that needs power to work, like a toy or a light? Where does the power come from?
  • When you do something kind for someone, who do you think helped you want to do that?

Say It Out Loud!

God is working inside me! I cooperate with what He is already doing! I walk with Jesus, and THAT means I win!

Amen!

Family Activity

Draw a lightbulb together. Inside the bulb, draw something kind you want to do today. Then draw a little lightning bolt next to it and label it GOD'S POWER!

Parent Note

Philippians 2:13 is the foundation of the entire passage. Paul distinguishes cooperation from effort. God supplies both the desire and the doing. Help your child understand they are not the energy source.

2

Day 2

No Grumbling!

The Bible Says

“Do all things without grumbling or disputing.”

Philippians 2:14, ESV

A long, long time ago, God's people were walking through a hot desert. God gave them food that fell from the sky every morning called manna! He gave them water from a rock. He kept them safe. But do you know what they did? They grumbled. They complained. They said this is too hard!

And all that complaining kept them from getting to the beautiful place God had for them. They grumbled themselves out of the good thing!

Paul says: do not be like that. Instead, be like a star! Stars shine in the dark. They do not wait for the dark to go away before they shine. They just burn and burn and burn. You can be a star shiner! When things are hard, you can still say: God is good. He has not forgotten me.

Let's Talk About It

  • What is something you sometimes grumble about? It is okay to be honest!
  • What could you say instead of grumbling to remind yourself God is good?

Say It Out Loud!

I do not grumble about hard things! I shine like a star wherever God puts me! I walk with Jesus, and THAT means I win!

Amen!

Family Activity

This week, when someone starts to grumble, say together: star or black hole? A star shines in the dark. A black hole pulls everyone into complaint. Choose to be a star!

Parent Note

The Greek word gongusmoi is the same word the Septuagint uses for Israel in the wilderness. Paul is invoking that whole narrative as a warning. Every grumble is a theological statement. Help your child see the connection between complaint and unbelief.

3

Day 3

Being Poured Out

The Bible Says

“Even if I am to be poured out as a drink offering upon the sacrifice and offering of your faith, I am glad and rejoice with you all.”

Philippians 2:17, ESV

Have you ever poured water for someone who was thirsty? When you pour the water out, it is gone from the cup. But it helped someone!

Paul says his life is like being poured out. He gives everything he has to help other people know Jesus. And here is the amazing part: he says he is GLAD about it! Not sad. Glad!

When you share your snack with a friend even though you really wanted all of it, that is being poured out. When you let your little brother or sister go first even though you wanted to go, that is being poured out. And the secret Paul knows is: being poured out for others makes your heart feel FULL, not empty.

Let's Talk About It

  • Can you think of a time you gave something away or helped someone and it made you feel good inside?
  • Who could you pour yourself out for this week?

Say It Out Loud!

I am willing to give to others! Being poured out for someone else makes me glad, not sad! I walk with Jesus, and THAT means I win!

Amen!

Family Activity

Together, pour water from one cup into another cup as you say: I give to others. Then talk about one way to pour kindness on someone this week. Make a plan and do it!

Parent Note

The nesek (drink offering) was secondary to the main sacrifice. Paul makes himself secondary to the Philippians' faith and does it with joy. Let your child experience the joy of giving something away this week and debrief how it felt afterward.

4

Day 4

Timothy Cared for Others

The Bible Says

“For I have no one like him, who will be genuinely concerned for your welfare.”

Philippians 2:20, ESV

Timothy was a special helper of Paul's. Paul said there was no one else quite like him because Timothy really, truly, genuinely cared about other people. Not just pretending to care. Really caring.

Paul said everyone else in the room was busy thinking about themselves. But Timothy was thinking about others. Paul could trust Timothy to go to the church and take care of those people the way Paul himself would.

Did you know you can be like Timothy? When your friend is sad at school, you can stop thinking about your own game and go ask how they are doing. When someone is left out, you can be the one who notices. That is what caring looks like. Jesus cared about people that way. And you can too!

Let's Talk About It

  • Is there a friend at school or church who might be feeling sad or left out right now?
  • What is one way you could show them you care, like Timothy did?

Say It Out Loud!

I genuinely care about other people! I notice when someone needs a friend! I walk with Jesus, and THAT means I win!

Amen!

Family Activity

Role-play together: What would you say to a friend who was left out at lunch? Practice caring out loud. Then write one person's name on a paper and put it somewhere you will see it this week as a reminder to show them care.

Parent Note

Paul used the word isopsuchos (equal-souled) only for Timothy in all of Scripture. His greatness was not gifting but proven character. Talk with your child about someone at school or church who might be lonely, and make a plan together to show care.

5

Day 5

Push All the Chips In!

The Bible Says

“He nearly died for the work of Christ, risking his life.”

Philippians 2:30, ESV

Epaphroditus (say it with me: eh-PAF-ro-DIE-tus!) was a brave man. He traveled a very long way across a whole sea to help Paul while Paul was in prison. He got very, very sick doing it. He almost died. But he kept going because he loved God and he wanted to help.

When he started getting better, do you know what he was worried about? He was worried that his friends back home were worried about him! He almost died, and he was thinking about how other people were feeling. That is the most unselfish heart!

Paul says Epaphroditus pushed ALL his chips in for Jesus. Not halfway. All the way. He gave everything. And God remembers that. God never forgets the people who go all in for Him.

Let's Talk About It

  • Is there something you only do halfway because it feels hard or scary?
  • What would it look like to go all in for Jesus in that thing?

Say It Out Loud!

I go all in for Jesus! I close the distance and I do not watch from the sidelines! I walk with Jesus, and THAT means I win!

Amen!

Family Activity

Play a simple card or board game together. When someone goes all in or takes a big risk, stop the game and say: that is what Epaphroditus did for Jesus! Celebrate the brave choice together.

Parent Note

The word paraboleuomai (risking his life) appears only here in the New Testament. It described gambling everything on one bet. Ask your child where God is asking them to close a distance this week. Make it specific and help them take one step.
