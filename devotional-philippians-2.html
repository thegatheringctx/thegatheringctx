Philippians · Week 2 Devotional

The Mind That Emptied Itself

Jesus had every right to stay in heaven. He chose to come down. All the way down. That choice is your invitation.

Philippians 2:1–11

Paul has been writing from a prison cell. Last week he told us about joy in chains. He told us that chains advance the gospel. He told us that for him to live is Christ and to die is gain.

Now he turns a corner.

He has heard something from Epaphroditus, the man who carried this letter to Philippi. There is a fracture in the Philippian church. Not a scandal. Not heresy. Something more subtle and more dangerous: pride. Selfish ambition. Believers looking out for themselves at the expense of each other.

And Paul's answer is not a policy. It is not a conflict resolution strategy. His answer is a Person.

Look at Jesus, he says. Not as a distant example. Not as an inspiration to post on the wall. As the mind you are invited to carry. The same orientation. The same posture. The same willingness to move downward when the world says move up.

01

The Ground on Which Unity Stands

"Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count others more significant than yourselves. Let each of you look not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others."

Philippians 2:3–4 · ESV · ESV

Paul names the exact culprits. Selfish ambition, eritheia in Greek, originally described a worker who served purely for personal gain, with no loyalty to the work itself. Conceit, kenodoxia, empty glory. The pursuit of a reputation disconnected from reality.

Paul says: do nothing from those two roots. Not less. Not fewer. Nothing.

What he puts in their place is tapeinophrosyne, humility of mind. In the Roman world this word was an insult. To call someone humble was to call them a groveler. Paul takes that insult and turns it into a virtue. True humility is not thinking less of yourself. It is thinking of yourself less, because you are so secure in who you are that you do not need to manage your image.

That security only comes from one place: knowing who God says you are.

Humility is not thinking less of yourself. It is thinking of yourself less. Secure identity is the soil from which unity grows.

Reflect:
1. Whose interests are you being asked to count more significant than your own?
2. Where does selfish ambition quietly show up in you?
3. What is one way you can look to another person's interests today?

Reflect

Where has selfish ambition been dressing itself up as conviction in you?

Name one person whose interests you will actually put ahead of your own this week.

02

The God Who Let Go

"Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus, who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men."

Philippians 2:5–7 · ESV · ESV

Paul says: have this mind. Not admire it. Not aspire to it eventually. Have it. Now.

Then he shows you what the mind looks like in the only Person who ever perfectly possessed it.

Jesus was in the morphe of God, not appearance, not costume, but the essential nature and very substance of what God is. Not like God. God. And He had equality with God, isos in Greek, identical in weight and measure.

A Being with that nature decided to let go.

The word Paul uses is kenōo, to empty, to pour out, to make of no effect. This is the kenosis of Christ. Jesus did not cease to be God. What He set aside was the independent exercise of His divine rights and privileges. He chose the limits of human flesh. He chose hunger and thirst and weariness and grief. He chose the cross when He could have called ten thousand angels.

He took the form of a doulos. A slave. The lowest social rank in the Roman world. The God who made the law became subject to the law.

Jesus did not lose His identity when He became a servant. He demonstrated it. He was most fully Himself at the basin, at the table, and at the cross.

Reflect:
1. What status or right do you hold tightly that Jesus willingly let go?
2. Where is God inviting you to take the form of a servant?
3. How does Christ emptying Himself reshape what greatness means to you?

Reflect

What are you holding onto that Jesus would have laid down?

He had the most to grasp and grasped nothing. What does that expose about your grip?

03

The Descent Was the Point

"And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross."

Philippians 2:8 · ESV · ESV

One verse. The most costly verse in the Bible.

Paul marks the descent step by step. God becomes human. Human humbles himself. Obedient to death. Not just death, death on a cross.

For a Roman audience this was almost unbearable. Crucifixion was not just an execution. It was a statement. Reserved for slaves and criminals. Designed to be maximally painful and maximally humiliating.

He did not drift into the incarnation. He did not stumble into the cross. He went there on purpose. Every step downward was a step chosen.

The descent was not a detour from the mission. The descent was the mission. And your low places are not where God's plan breaks down. They may be exactly where the plan is being fulfilled.

The road to exaltation runs through the low place. God does not skip the valley. He transforms it.

Reflect:
1. Where are you avoiding a humbling that God may be calling you into?
2. What does obedience to the point of death cost you right now?
3. How does the cross change the way you view lowly, unseen faithfulness?

Reflect

Where do you want the reward without the descent?

Jesus went all the way down, obedient to death on a cross. What is the lowest step you have been unwilling to take?

04

Therefore

"Therefore God has highly exalted him and bestowed on him the name that is above every name, so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father."

Philippians 2:9–11 · ESV · ESV

There is one word you cannot skip. Therefore.

Therefore connects what came before to what comes next. The exaltation is the direct consequence of the descent. You do not get from verse 6 to verse 9 without going through verses 7 and 8. Down before up. Servant before Lord. Cross before crown.

The Greek word Paul uses is huperupsoo, hyper-exaltation. Not just lifted up. Lifted to a position no created being has ever occupied.

What name? Lord. Kyrios in Greek. But behind that stands the Hebrew, YHWH. The name of God too sacred for Jewish readers to even pronounce. Paul is saying Jesus has been given that name. Not as a new addition. As a revelation of what was always true.

Every knee. In heaven. On earth. Under the earth. There is no realm of reality excluded. It is not a question of if. Only when.

The exaltation was not the reversal of the humility. It was the completion of it. The wounds are still in the risen body. The humility was not a phase. It was the very thing that qualified the exaltation.

If you are in Christ, co-heir with the One who went all the way down, the therefore is coming for you too.

Reflect:
1. What descent in your life might God be planning to lift?
2. Where are you grasping for exaltation instead of trusting God to give it?
3. What would it look like to let God do the lifting?

Reflect

Are you campaigning for your own name in a room where God alone does the lifting?

What changes if you trust the therefore and stop promoting yourself?

05

This Mind Is Already Yours

"Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus."

Philippians 2:5 · ESV · ESV

Paul does not say try to develop this mind. Work toward it. Earn it. He says have this mind, which is yours in Christ Jesus.

The mind of Christ is not something you manufacture. It is something you receive. It is already yours in Him.

You are not reaching toward something foreign to your nature. You are becoming more of what you already are in Christ. The new creation is not your old self polished. It is the life of Jesus taking up residence in a human body and expressing itself through it. Union. Not imitation.

The same mind that emptied itself of every divine right and chose a servant's death for you, that mind is yours. Open it. Use it. Let it reorient every room you walk into this week.

Reflect:
1. The mind of Christ is already yours in Him. Where do you need to live it out?
2. What thought pattern needs to bow to the mind of Christ today?
3. Who in your life needs you to have this mind toward them?

Reflect

Do you treat the mind of Christ as something to manufacture or as something you already have?

Where have you been striving for what has already been given to you?

Reflect on the Week

Where in your life are you still grasping, holding onto a position, recognition, or reputation that God may be asking you to release?

What would it look like this week to genuinely count someone around you as more significant than yourself, not as a performance, but as an overflow of who you are in Christ?

You are a co-heir with the One who went all the way down and was exalted to the highest place. How does that change the way you walk into the low places in your own life right now?

A Prayer of Identity

Father, I receive what You have given me. The mind of Christ is mine, not because I earned it, but because I am in Him. I open my hands today. I release the grip on the positions, the recognition, the status I have been holding. Not because I have to. Because Jesus showed me that the hands that give freely are the ones You fill. I am secure enough to count others more significant. I am loved enough to choose the low place freely. I trust the therefore. I trust that the descent is not the end. You do not leave the valley as the final chapter. You transform it. I am in You, and You are in me, and that is all the security I will ever need. I walk with victory, not to it, in Jesus' name. Amen.

Declaration

I have the mind of Christ.
I do not grasp for status. I release it.
I do not compete for significance. I carry it already.
Jesus emptied Himself and God exalted Him,
and I am a co-heir with Christ.
I count others more significant not because I am less,
but because I am secure.
I choose the low place because the God of the high place has already claimed me.
Every knee will bow and every tongue will confess
that Jesus Christ is Lord.
And that Lord is my Lord.
I walk with victory, not to it.

Now go with the mind of Christ. May the God who emptied Himself of all rights to fill you with all grace keep you from grasping what was never yours to hold. May the Father who chose the low place show you where the low places in your own life are and give you the courage and the security to choose them freely. May you walk into your home this week and look for who needs to be counted more significant. May you walk into every hard relationship with your hands open instead of your fists clenched. And when the world cannot figure out why you serve without demanding credit, why you give without keeping score, why you bend when everything in you says stand your ground, may you have the simple confidence to say: I have the mind of Christ.
