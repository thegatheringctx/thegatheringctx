Ephesians · Week 5 Devotional

Walk in Love, Light, Wisdom

Love, light, wisdom, and a marriage that preaches.

Ephesians 5

Paul has spent four chapters telling you who you are. Chosen before the foundation of the world. Adopted. Raised. Seated. Built together into a dwelling place for God by the Spirit. He has not asked you to earn one word of it.

Now he turns the corner. Ephesians 5:1 to 33 is not a list of rules stapled onto the gospel. It is the gospel learning to walk around in an ordinary week. Imitate God. Walk in love. Walk in the light. Walk carefully. Stay filled. And let your home preach. Notice the order Paul never breaks. Identity first. Behavior second. He does not say become a beloved child by walking in love. He says walk in love because you already are one.

Remember the city this letter landed in. Ephesus was a harbor town of temples, traffic, and appetite. The temple of Artemis pulled pilgrims from across the empire. Wine was poured in the name of a god who promised that emptiness could be filled if you drank enough of it. Marriage was mostly a contract, and in most Roman households the wife had no voice in it at all. Into that city Paul writes that holiness is not a burden laid on slaves. It is the family resemblance of beloved children. The names have changed. The city has not. Walk this week as who you already are.

01

Imitate the Father, Walk in Love

Therefore be imitators of God, as beloved children. And walk in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God.

Ephesians 5:1 to 7 · ESV

Imitate God. Sit with how outrageous that command is. Not imitate a good pastor. Not imitate a respected elder. Not imitate the most disciplined believer you know. Imitate God. And before the weight of it crushes you, Paul slides the foundation underneath it: as beloved children. You are not auditioning for the family. You are already in it. The command is not a ladder. It is a mirror held up to a child who already carries the Father's name.

Children do not imitate by straining. They imitate by proximity. No child wakes up and decides to mimic a parent through concentrated effort. They simply absorb it. The walk. The tone of voice. The way of handling a hard moment. They absorb it because they are near, because they are loved, because they belong. That is the whole method. Not performance. Proximity. And Paul immediately tells you what the family resemblance looks like: walk in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us. A fragrant offering. A sacrifice. That is altar language, straight out of Leviticus, the smoke of the burnt offering rising and God saying it pleases Me. The love of God is not sentimental. It is self giving. It does not scan a room for what it can collect. It looks for what it can lay down.

Greek Word: mimētēs (μιμητής), an imitator, the word our English word mimic comes from. It carries the idea of careful, sustained, intentional modeling of another person's character. This is discipleship at its deepest level, staying close to God long enough that His ways become your reflex instead of your effort.

Then Paul names what has to go. Sexual immorality. All impurity. Covetousness. Filthiness. Foolish talk. Crude joking. Must not even be named among you. At a Greco Roman banquet, coarse talk was the entertainment, and covetousness was simply called ambition. Paul refuses to grade on that curve. He is not shaming you. He is reminding you. That is not who you are anymore. Stop calling it normal. And do not let anyone deceive you with empty words that say conduct does not matter because grace covers it. Grace is not permission to stay. Grace is the power to leave. When prayer feels heavy and worship feels far off, it is often not because God moved. It is because we made room for something that no longer belongs to us. Put it down. Not in condemnation. In love. The grace to do it is already in you.

Reflect

You imitate whoever you spend the most time watching. Who is that right now, honestly?

Where does your love look like giving yourself up, and where does it look like getting?

02

You Were Darkness. Now You Are Light.

for at one time you were darkness, but now you are light in the Lord. Walk as children of light.

Ephesians 5:8 to 14 · ESV

Read Paul's grammar slowly, because he does not say what you expect him to say. He does not say you were in darkness. He says you were darkness. And he does not say you are now in the light, or that you are getting brighter with practice. He says you are light in the Lord. That is not a compliment. That is a verdict about your nature. The One who is Light moved in, and what He is, you now are. Identity again, before a single instruction arrives.

Greek Word: phōs (φῶς), light. In the ancient world light meant far more than illumination. It meant truth, revelation, and the presence of the divine. God is light. And darkness in this letter is never merely the absence of brightness. It is a domain, a realm of deception and bondage with a will behind it. To walk as children of light is not to project a good attitude. It is to carry a Person into every room you enter.

Light does two things. It reveals, and it produces. Paul says take no part in the unfruitful works of darkness, but instead expose them. Notice how. Not by arguing. Not by launching a campaign. Not by becoming the most outraged person on the internet. By shining. A flashlight does not debate the darkness. It simply turns on. And light produces. The fruit of light is found in all that is good and right and true. You do not manufacture that fruit by gritting your teeth. It grows in you because of where you are standing. The world says expose the darkness by shouting at it. Paul says expose it by living in the light where it cannot survive.

Then verse 14 breaks into poetry, and most scholars hear an early Christian hymn here, the kind of thing the church may well have sung over new believers coming up out of the baptismal water. Awake, O sleeper, and arise from the dead, and Christ will shine on you. That is an altar call hiding inside the theology. Somebody reading this is not dead. Somebody reading this is asleep. Drifted. Numbed by the slow anesthesia of routine and compromise and making quiet peace with the wrong things. Present in the room but not producing fruit. Alive but not awake. Hear it as it was sung. Awake. The light of Christ is already shining on you. You do not have to stay in the drift. Arise.

Reflect

Paul does not say you were in darkness. He says you were darkness, and now you are light. Do you believe the second half as firmly as the first?

What is one hidden thing you need to bring into the light this week?

03

Look Carefully How You Walk

Look carefully then how you walk, not as unwise but as wise, making the best use of the time, because the days are evil.

Ephesians 5:15 to 17 · ESV

Paul shifts the question. For seven verses he has told you what to walk away from. Now he asks how you walk. And he chooses a word that will not let you stay casual: carefully. Not reactively. Not by drifting along in whatever current everybody else is riding. Carefully. With precision. With your eyes open. This is not anxiety. This is attention.

He gives the reason plainly. Because the days are evil. That is not pessimism, and it is not a complaint about how bad the culture has gotten. It is discernment. The days you live in are not neutral ground. There are forces at work in this moment that are perfectly happy to take your marriage, your integrity, your attention, and your calling, one unnoticed inch at a time. Hear this clearly. An unintentional believer in an intentionally dark culture will lose ground without ever realizing it. Nobody drifts toward wisdom. Drift only runs one direction.

Greek Word: kairos (καιρός), the time, the season, the opportunity at hand. Paul is not handing you a secret code. He is pointing at the actual openings in front of you. And the participle he attaches to it, exagorazomenoi, is a marketplace word meaning to buy up, the way a merchant clears the stall of everything worth having before the day ends. Buy up the opportunity. The conversation you keep meaning to have. The apology you keep postponing. The person God keeps putting in your path. Those windows are finite. Do not sleepwalk past them.

And then verse 17. Understand what the will of the Lord is. Paul is not sending you on an anxious treasure hunt for a hidden map. He has spent five chapters telling you the will of God out loud. Walk worthy. Put off the old self. Speak truth. Forgive as you were forgiven. Walk in love. Walk in the light. Be filled with the Spirit. The world says find your purpose. Paul says live aligned with what has already been written. The will of God is not hidden from you. Most of it is simply waiting on you.

Reflect

Where are you walking reactively instead of carefully?

Name one decision in front of you that deserves more thought than you have been giving it.

04

Be Being Filled

And do not get drunk with wine, for that is debauchery, but be filled with the Spirit.

Ephesians 5:18 to 21 · ESV

This is where Paul's practical teaching reaches its summit, and he gets there by way of a contrast his readers could see out their front door. Ephesus knew the cult of Dionysus. Wine was not just a drink there. It was a doorway. The promise was that if you drank deep enough, the god would fill you, and for one night the emptiness would go quiet. Paul looks at that whole arrangement and says the hunger is real, the door is wrong. The world says fill yourself and the ache will stop. Paul says be filled by Someone and the ache will be answered. The names have changed. The ache has not.

Greek Word: plēroō (πληρόω), to fill. In verse 18 it stands as a present passive imperative, which is the single most important observation in this whole chapter. Present means continuous, not once. Passive means done to you, not by you. Imperative means commanded, not optional. Be being filled. It is the same word Paul already prayed over you in Ephesians 3:19, filled with all the fullness of God. You do not fill yourself with the Spirit. You yield to the Spirit who is already in you. The overflow is His work. Your part is surrender.

So stop treating the Spirit as a weekend presence. He does not only fill the Sunday moments. He fills the Monday ordinary. The commute. The kitchen. The hard conversation you have been rehearsing. The quiet morning nobody sees. He is a continuously available, inexhaustible reservoir of everything you actually need, and the reason so many believers run dry is not that the well is low. It is that we only go to it in emergencies.

Watch what Paul says a filled life produces, because he does not describe a feeling. He describes four evidences, and every one of them shows up in a room with other people. Song, addressing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs. Heart level worship, making melody to the Lord with your heart, which means this goes deeper than the volume of your voice. Gratitude, giving thanks always and for everything. And mutual submission, submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ. That last one is the test. You can fake the singing. You cannot fake preferring somebody else when it costs you. Hold on to verse 21. Tomorrow the whole marriage passage hangs on it.

Reflect

What do you reach for to take the edge off, and what does that reaching tell you about where you look for filling?

Filled is continuous, not once. What would being filled this week actually cost you?

05

Your Marriage Is a Sermon

Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her.

Ephesians 5:22 to 33 · ESV

A lot of people hit these verses and tense up. Some check out. Some file it away as a product of its time. Stay in the room, because Paul is not building a power ladder that props up husbands and shrinks wives. He is doing something far more radical than his culture could hold. In the Roman household code, the head of the house held nearly absolute authority, and instruction was written to him about his property. Paul turns and speaks directly to wives, to children, and to slaves as full moral agents with their own standing before Christ. That alone was startling. Then he tells the man with all the power to spend it, all of it, on the good of the one he was expected to rule. And look at where Paul puts the weight. Wives get three verses. Husbands get nine. Read that again, husband. The demand Paul lays on you is three times heavier than anything he asks of her. The problem in most struggling marriages is not that wives are failing to submit. It is that husbands have never counted what Christlike love actually costs.

Get the structure right and the passage stops being frightening. It all hangs on verse 21, submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ, and the participle there is hypotassō, a word for voluntarily arranging yourself under another, willing deference rather than coerced servitude. In the earliest manuscripts verse 22 carries no verb of its own. It reaches back and borrows that one. So what Paul asks of a wife is not a separate category of subjugation. It is one expression of the Spirit filled posture he just handed the entire church. And hear this plainly, because it matters. Paul is not asking wives to submit to every man, or to a husband who has not earned it, or to abuse or neglect. He is describing life inside a healthy, Christ centered covenant where a husband is already serving his wife the way Christ serves the church. If you are in a marriage where you are being harmed, this text is not a chain to hold you there. Get to safety, and reach out to me or to a trusted leader at The Gathering CTX at info@gatheringctx.org. You do not have to carry that alone.

Greek Word: agapaō (ἀγαπάω), to love. Not eros, romantic feeling, and not phileo, warm affection between friends. Agape is the deliberate, covenantal choosing of another person's good above your own. This is not a love that waits until you feel it. It is a love that acts consistently and sacrificially whether or not it is returned, and whether or not anybody notices.

How did Christ love the church? He gave Himself up for her. He washed her. He is actively working to present her in splendor, holy and without blemish. That is not passive affection. That is not providing financially and calling it love. That is not occasional kindness with an invoice attached. Paul adds two words in verse 29 that ought to reorganize a man's week. Nourish, to bring to full development, to invest in her growth until she becomes everything God made her to be. Cherish, literally to warm, to hold something precious close and shield it from harm. So ask it honestly. Is she flourishing because of how you love her? Is she more secure, more seen, more known? Does she know in the private places of your home that you are for her? And then Paul quotes Genesis 2:24 and tells you the secret that was hidden in the oldest institution on earth. This mystery is profound, and it refers to Christ and the church. From the beginning, God was writing a story about a Bridegroom who would leave His Father's house, pursue a bride who was not worthy of Him, give Himself entirely for her, and make her one with Himself. Your marriage was never merely private. It is a living sermon. Make sure it is preaching the right gospel.

Reflect

If your marriage preaches a sermon about Christ and the church, what has it been preaching lately?

Husbands, Paul puts the weight on you first. Where have you used authority instead of laying yourself down? And if your home is a place of harm, this passage condemns the one doing the harming. Tell a pastor. Do not carry that quietly.

Reflect on the Week

Where in your ordinary week, not your spiritual highlight reel, would imitating the Father actually change what you do next?

Is there something you have quietly renamed as normal that Paul says must not even be named among saints?

Are you awake or asleep right now? If Christ is already shining on you, what would arising look like before Sunday?

Be being filled is continuous, not occasional. What does yielding to the Spirit look like in your Monday, not just your Sunday?

If you are married, what is your marriage preaching about Christ and the church this week? If you are not, who in your life needs the kind of self giving love described here?

A Prayer of Identity

Father, I am Your beloved child. I did not earn that and I cannot lose it, so I stop striving and I come close.

Teach me to imitate You the way a child does, by proximity and not by performance. Give me love that gives itself away. Whatever I have made peace with that no longer belongs to me, I put it down today. Not because I am afraid of You, but because I am Yours.

I was darkness. Now I am light in the Lord. Wake me up where I have drifted. Let me stop arguing with the darkness and simply shine. Help me walk carefully, buying up every opportunity You set in front of me instead of sleepwalking past it.

Fill me, Lord, and keep filling me. Not once. Continuously. In the car, in the kitchen, in the hard conversation, in the quiet morning nobody sees. I cannot manufacture the overflow. I can only surrender, so I surrender now.

And for my home, Lord, make it a picture of Christ and His church. Where I have failed to love, forgive me and rebuild me. Where I have been wounded, heal me and protect me. Let my life preach the gospel to everyone who watches it. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Declaration

I am an imitator of God as His beloved child.

I walk in love, the kind that gives itself up.

I was darkness. I am light in the Lord, and I walk as a child of light.

I make no peace with the works of darkness. I expose them by shining.

I am awake. Not drifting, not sleeping, not numb. Alive and producing fruit.

I am filled with the Spirit continuously, not occasionally, and that filling overflows in worship, in gratitude, and in love for the people around me.

I walk carefully, buying up every moment God has handed me.

My home is a living picture of Christ and His church, and I take that calling seriously.

I walk with victory, not to it, in Jesus' name. Amen.

Now go. Walking in love. Walking in the light. Walking in wisdom. Walking filled.

May the God who is your Father remind you this week that you are beloved, and that beloved children walk in the nature of their Father without straining to.

May the Spirit of God fill every ordinary moment you have. The car. The kitchen. The hard conversation. The quiet morning. And may His overflow produce in you what you could never manufacture yourself. Gratitude. Song. Gentleness. Love.

For those of you who are married, may your home become what God designed it to be. May husbands love with the costly, nourishing, cherishing love of Christ. May wives honor with the settled confidence of a bride who is safe. And may everyone around you wonder what makes your home different.

Awake, O sleeper. Arise. Christ is shining on you. In the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Amen.
