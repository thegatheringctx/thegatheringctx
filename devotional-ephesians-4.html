Ephesians · Week 4

Walk Worthy

Ephesians 4:1–16
May 3, 2026
Scripture Reading

"I therefore, a prisoner for the Lord, urge you to walk in a manner worthy of the calling to which you have been called, with all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love, eager to maintain the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace."— Ephesians 4:1–3

01

From Identity to Conduct

The first three chapters of Ephesians are almost entirely about what God has done for us. Chosen. Adopted. Redeemed. Seated. Revealed. Filled.

Chapter 4 opens with the word therefore. And in the Bible, when you see therefore you ask: what is it there for? The answer is everything Paul has just said.

Therefore, because of all of this, walk worthy of the calling to which you have been called.

The shift from Ephesians 1–3 to Ephesians 4–6 is the shift from identity to conduct. From who you are to how you walk. From the indicative to the imperative. From the gift to the responsibility.

God does not give you identity so you can sit still. He gives you identity so you have something worth walking in.

"You are not walking worthy to earn grace. You are walking worthy because of grace."
02

Walk in a Way That Matches Your Name

Worthy does not mean perfect. It means proportionate. It means your walk matches your talk. Your conduct matches your confession. Your Monday matches your Sunday.

You carry the name of Jesus Christ. You are a son of God. You are a member of His body. You are a royal priesthood. Walk in a way that is proportionate to that name.

This is not performance based Christianity. It is identity based living. You are not walking worthy to earn grace. You are walking worthy because of grace.

axios (αξιος)
"Worthy," from the root meaning to weigh. It is the image of a scale in balance. Walk axios means walk in a way that balances, that is proportionate to the weight of what God has called you to. Your daily life should carry the same weight as your identity in Christ. Not heavier. Not lighter. Balanced.
03

Unity Is Maintained, Not Created

"Bearing with one another in love, eager to maintain the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace."

Notice: maintain, not create. The Spirit has already produced the unity. Your job is to keep it. To guard it. To refuse to let division rob what the Spirit has built.

And the way you maintain it is specific: with all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love. This is not passive. It is active. It requires humility, which is the most countercultural virtue in a self promoting culture.

The reason Paul pushes so hard for unity is the foundation he gives in verses 4–6: one body, one Spirit, one hope, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all. Seven ones. You cannot be unified in seven dimensions and divided in your pew.

spoudazō (σπουδαζω)
"Eager, diligent, making every effort," used in Ephesians 4:3. This word carries urgency. It is not casual. It is not optional. Paul is saying: urgently, earnestly, with your whole effort, maintain the unity. Division in the body of Christ is not just a social problem. It is a spiritual emergency.
"Unity is not accidental. It is maintained by people who choose humility over being right."
04

Gifts Build the Body, Not the Platform

Christ gave gifts to His people: apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds, teachers. Not so that certain people could be stars, but so that the saints might be equipped for the work of ministry, for the building up of the body of Christ.

The fivefold gifts exist for equipping. Not performing. Not platforming. Equipping.

A minister who uses their gift to build a following rather than build the body has missed the point entirely. The gifts are tools, and tools exist to build something greater than themselves.

And every member has a gift. Not just the platform ministers. Every believer has been given something, and the body only reaches full maturity when every part is functioning in its right place.

05

Maturity Means Being Anchored

The goal of all the gifts, all the equipping, all the building is the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ. Full spiritual maturity.

And Paul contrasts maturity with immaturity in vivid terms: children tossed by waves and carried about by every wind of doctrine, by human cunning, by craftiness in deceitful schemes.

Spiritual immaturity is not primarily a knowledge problem. It is an anchor problem. The immature believer has not yet planted deep enough in Christ to hold firm when the wind comes. And wind will come. False doctrine will come. Deception will come. Persecution will come.

The mature believer is not unmoved by storms. They are unswept by them. Roots deep enough to bend without breaking.

Maturity is not knowing more. It is being anchored enough that storms cannot sweep you away.

Reflect

Questions for the Week

  • Does your Monday match your Sunday? Where is your walk out of balance with your identity?
  • Are you actively maintaining unity, or passively allowing division to grow?
  • Is your gift being used to build the body or to build your own platform?
  • What winds of doctrine or circumstance have been tossing you lately? Where do you need deeper roots?
  • What would it look like for you to take your place in the body and function in your gift faithfully?
Pray

Father, thank You for my identity in Christ. Help me walk worthy of the calling You have placed on my life. Give me humility to maintain unity with my brothers and sisters. Show me where my gift fits in the body and help me use it to build others up, not myself. Anchor me deep in You so that no wind of doctrine or storm of life can sweep me away. I want to grow up in every way into Christ. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

Declare

I walk worthy of my calling! I maintain the unity of the Spirit! I am not tossed by every wind of doctrine. I am rooted and grounded in Christ! My gift is not for my platform. It is for the building of the body! I am growing up in every way into Him who is the head! I walk with victory, not to it. Amen!
