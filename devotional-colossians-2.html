Colossians · Week 2 Devotional

All of Him

Every power you fear, He made.

Colossians 1:15 to 23

This week we climb the summit of the letter. Many believe Colossians 1:15 to 20 was an early hymn of the church, a song believers were already singing within a generation of the resurrection. Paul lifts it up and preaches it to a church that had been told Jesus was one power among many, with higher authorities above Him you had to climb past and appease.

Paul answers fear the same way the early church did. Not with reassurance. With a portrait. Five days this week, we look at one Person until every rival shrinks. Everything you are afraid of is answered by who Jesus is.

01

The Image of the Invisible God

He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation.

Colossians 1:15 · ESV

If you want to see the Father's heart, watch the Son.

Every human heart wonders the same thing: what is God actually like? Is He distant? Disappointed? Watching for me to fail? Paul answers in five words. Jesus is the image of the invisible God. Not a copy. Not a lesser version. The visible display of the God you cannot see.

So watch Jesus. He touches a leper nobody else will touch. He weeps at a friend's tomb. He welcomes children when the adults try to shoo them away. He takes up a cross for people who abandoned him. That is not a softer side of God. That is the very heart of God on display.

Greek Word: eikōn (εἰκών), image, exact representation, the visible display of what is unseen. Hebrews says the Son is the exact imprint of God's nature. In Genesis, humanity was made in God's image and we shattered it. Christ does not merely bear the image. He is the image, and in chapter three Paul will say you are being renewed after the image of your Creator. The One who is the Image is remaking you.

Reflect:
1. What picture of God did you grow up with? Where does it match Jesus, and where does it not?
2. Read one story about Jesus today (try Mark 1:40 to 45) and finish this sentence: So this is what God is like.
3. If Christ is remaking you after His own image, what is one place you can already see it?

Reflect

What have you assumed God feels about you when you are not doing well?

Now look at Jesus. Where exactly does that assumption fall apart?

02

The Load Bearing Wall

For by him all things were created, in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities, all things were created through him and for him. And he is before all things, and in him all things hold together.

Colossians 1:16 to 17 · ESV

Your fear is not too big. It is just aimed at something too small.

Colossae feared the powers. Thrones, dominions, rulers, authorities, unseen forces they believed ran the world. Paul names those exact powers and says Jesus made them. They are not His rivals. They are His handiwork. You cannot be created if you created everything.

And do not think Colossae is far away. We build our own chain of powers. Jesus and the election. Jesus and the economy. Jesus and my healing. Jesus and my kid coming home. Anything you have to add to Jesus before your soul can rest is a throne you have built over Him.

Greek Word: synestēken (συνέστηκεν), holds together, coheres, stands united. From synistēmi. Paul says all things hold together in Christ, present tense, right now. He is not the retired architect who drew the plans and left. He is the load bearing wall holding the whole house up this second.

Reflect:
1. Name the power that feels bigger than you this week. Now say out loud: He made it. He outranks it. He is holding it together this second.
2. Finish the sentence honestly: Jesus and. What have you been requiring before your soul can rest?
3. Where have you treated Jesus as one resource among many instead of the One holding everything?

Reflect

Name the power you are actually afraid of.

Jesus made it, He outranks it, and He is holding it right now. What would this week look like if you believed that at the level of your nerves and not only your theology?

03

Fullness, Not Fractions

And he is the head of the body, the church. He is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, that in everything he might be preeminent. For in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell.

Colossians 1:18 to 19 · ESV

Stop climbing. In Him all the fullness of God lives, and He is already yours.

Watch the pivot. Paul has been in the heavens, and now he brings it down to the church. The same One who holds the galaxies together is the head over your congregation, your small group, your living room. You are not a footnote to His cosmic work. You are His body.

Then Paul says the thing the false teachers could not swallow. In Him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell. Not a portion. Not an emanation. The glory that once filled the tabernacle and the temple has taken up residence in a Person. There is no higher floor above Jesus, and there is no secret level of Christian above the one who simply has Him.

Greek Word: plērōma (πλήρωμα), fullness, the whole and undivided completeness. This word becomes the hinge of the whole letter. It returns in chapter two: in Christ the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily, and you have been filled in Him. The fullness does not live one floor up. It lives in Christ, and Christ lives in you.

Reflect:
1. Where have you been searching for something more than Christ, a higher tier, a secret, an experience you have not attained?
2. What would rest look like today if you believed you have already been filled in Him?
3. Pray Colossians 1:19 back to God and thank Him that nothing of God is missing from Jesus, and Jesus is not missing from you.

Reflect

What have you been climbing toward, hoping it would finally make you full?

If all the fullness of God dwells in Christ and Christ is already yours, what do you get to stop climbing?

04

Reconciled and Presented Blameless

And you, who once were alienated and hostile in mind, doing evil deeds, he has now reconciled in his body of flesh by his death, in order to present you holy and blameless and above reproach before him.

Colossians 1:21 to 22 · ESV

Not because of your record, but because of His.

Paul has been cosmic for six verses, thrones and fullness and the blood that reconciles all things. Then two words bring the whole galaxy down to your address. And you. The One holding the universe together reached into your hostility and made peace, in His body of flesh, by His death. The hands that flung the stars are the hands they drove the nails through.

And hear the goal. To present you holy and blameless and above reproach. That is altar language, the inspection of an offering with no spot and no defect. You did not pass the inspection. The spotless One took your place. When the Father looks at you in Christ, there is nothing left to accuse.

Greek Word: apokatallassō (ἀποκαταλλάσσω), to reconcile completely, to bring back from hostility into full peace. The word is intensified. Not a truce. A total bringing home. The distance you feel from God was closed at the cross. He did not lower the standard to reach you. He paid the debt to bring you all the way home.

Reflect:
1. Where does the accusation still play loudest in your head? Read verse 22 out loud over that exact place.
2. What is the difference between being tolerated and being presented holy, blameless, and above reproach?
3. Thank God today for one specific thing the cross closed: the distance, the debt, the verdict.

Reflect

Read your own name into verse 22. God will present me holy, blameless, and above reproach.

Which accusation loses its grip the moment that is true?

05

The Song That Fights Fear

...if indeed you continue in the faith, stable and steadfast, not shifting from the hope of the gospel that you heard, which has been proclaimed in all creation under heaven.

Colossians 1:23 · ESV

It is hard for fear and worship to live in the same heart.

Real faith holds on. The proof that you have been reconciled is not a perfect week. It is a heart that keeps holding to the gospel and will not shift off the hope you heard. Nobody renounces Jesus in a day. They drift one add on at a time, until the Jesus they are holding is too small to hold them. Stay planted.

And remember how this passage began its life. Many believe verses 15 to 20 were a song before they were a chapter. The early church fought fear by singing the supremacy of Jesus until fear had no room left. Paul commands it later in this very letter: psalms and hymns and spiritual songs. He sang one himself at midnight in a Philippian jail. When the wave hits, sing the One who is holding you.

Greek Word: hedraios (ἑδραῖος), seated, settled, firm, stable. Paul's word for the believer who continues in the faith. Not straining to hold on with white knuckles, but settled, like a foundation stone that does not move because of what it rests on. You are stable because He is.

Reflect:
1. Which direction have you been drifting lately, and what add on has been pulling you?
2. Put on one worship song today and sing it over the thing you named on Day 2. Out loud.
3. Read Colossians 1:15 to 20 out loud as a song of praise. Make the church's oldest confession your own: Jesus is Lord over all of it.

Reflect

Where has fear moved into a room where worship used to live?

Name one thing you will hold to this week when your feelings shift and the gospel does not.

A Prayer of Identity

Father, I have been aiming my fear at things You made. Forgive me for building thrones over Jesus, for the quiet add ons I have required before my soul would rest. Today I look at Your Son and I see You. I lay every power that feels bigger than me under His feet, because He made it, He outranks it, and He is holding it together right now. Thank You that the hands that flung the stars are the hands that were nailed to the cross for me. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Declaration

Jesus is the image of the invisible God. I do not have to guess what God is like.

Every power I fear, He made, He outranks, and He is holding right now.

In Him all the fullness of God dwells, and I have been filled in Him. I stop climbing.

I have been reconciled by the blood of His cross, and God will present me holy, blameless, and above reproach.

I do not add to Jesus. I draw all of life from Jesus.

I walk with victory, not to it, in Jesus' name. Amen.

Now go, and live under a bigger Jesus. May every power you fear shrink to its true size beneath the One who made it.

And now to him who is able to keep you from stumbling and to present you blameless before the presence of his glory with great joy, to the only God, our Savior, through Jesus Christ our Lord, be glory, majesty, dominion, and authority, before all time and now and forever.

In the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Amen.
