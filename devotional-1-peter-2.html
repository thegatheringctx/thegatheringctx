1 and 2 Peter: Living Hope · Week 2 Devotional

The Alien Life

Pressure does not create what is inside you. It reveals it.

1 Peter 2:1 to 25

Squeeze an orange and you get orange juice. Not apple juice. The pressure did not create anything. It revealed what was already inside.

That is the question 1 Peter 2 is really asking. When you get squeezed this week, when you are misunderstood, wrongly judged, or your buttons get pushed by someone who does not deserve the satisfaction, what comes out?

Peter is writing to people who were getting squeezed constantly. Elect exiles, he calls them, scattered across five Roman provinces in what is now Turkey. They had changed. They had walked away from the pagan practices woven into ordinary social life, and their neighbors read that as rebellion. They were not persecuted by an empire so much as pushed to the edges by everyone they knew. Outsiders in the only home they had.

Here is what Peter does not do. He does not hand them a behavior plan. He does not tell them to try harder to be good so people will like them again.

He tells them who they are.

That order is everything, and it is the spine of this whole chapter. Before Peter asks these people for a single thing, he tells them they are living stones built on a Living Stone. A chosen race. A royal priesthood. A holy nation. A people for his own possession. Once you were not a people, but now you are God's people. The conduct he calls for afterward is never how they earn that name. It is how they wear it.

So this week is not a self improvement plan. It is five days of learning a name you were already given, and letting that name be what comes out when the squeeze comes.

01

Crave What Feeds You

So put away all malice and all deceit and hypocrisy and envy and all slander. Like newborn infants, long for the pure spiritual milk, that by it you may grow up into salvation, if indeed you have tasted that the Lord is good.

1 Peter 2:1 to 3 · ESV

Notice the first word. So. Sometimes translated therefore. Peter is not starting a new thought, he is drawing a line from everything chapter 1 just said. You have been born again. You have been given new life through the living and abiding word of God. Because of that, so.

The being born again was something done to you. You did not perform it. Now comes your part, and it is active. Put away.

Look at what he lists. Malice, deceit, hypocrisy, envy, slander. Five things, and they are not a random sampling of sins. Every one of them poisons relationship. Malice poisons the heart before a word is spoken. Deceit corrupts how you communicate. Hypocrisy fractures your integrity so that who you are in the room does not match who you are at home. Envy quietly turns other people's good news into your bad day. Slander finishes the job and takes the community down with it.

Peter is clearing ground. These five are not just bad, they are appetite killers. You cannot hunger for the pure spiritual milk while you are full of them.

Then the image turns tender. Like newborn infants, long for the pure spiritual milk. Anyone who has been near a hungry baby knows there is nothing polite about it. That hunger is not scheduled, negotiated or embarrassed. It is instinct.

That is what Peter says your appetite for God's word is supposed to look like. Not a discipline you grind out. An instinct.

And he grounds it in something already true of you: if indeed you have tasted that the Lord is good. He is reaching back to Psalm 34:8, oh, taste and see that the LORD is good. This is not if you are lucky enough to have tasted. It is you have tasted, so of course you want more.

That by it you may grow up into salvation. You are already saved. Chapter 1 settled that. What Peter wants is for you to grow all the way up into the salvation you already have.

Reflect

Which of the five does your heart reach for most easily when you are hurt? Malice, deceit, hypocrisy, envy, or slander?

Is your appetite for God's word currently an instinct or a discipline you are forcing? What has been dulling it?

You have tasted that the Lord is good. When was the last time you let yourself remember what that tasted like?

02

Come to the Living Stone

As you come to him, a living stone rejected by men but in the sight of God chosen and precious, you yourselves like living stones are being built up as a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.

1 Peter 2:4 to 8 · ESV

Peter says stone five times in five verses. He wants you to trip over the word.

Start with the strangeness of it. A living stone. Stone is the deadest, most inert thing on the ancient jobsite. It sits where you put it. Peter takes that word and attaches life to it, because he is describing a Man who was quarried, rejected, thrown on the discard pile by the professionals, and then walked out of a tomb.

Rejected by men but in the sight of God chosen and precious. Both are true at the same time, and Peter is handing that to people who felt discarded by their own neighbors. The verdict of the room is not the verdict of God. It was not for Jesus, and it is not for you.

Then watch what he does. You yourselves like living stones. Same phrase. The life in the cornerstone runs into the stones built on him. Not because there is life in you naturally, but because of what you are joined to.

Now hear where the building is going. Being built up as a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood. That word for priesthood is not about your personal spiritual rank. It is collective. Peter is not saying each of you is a priest with a private office. He is saying together you are a priesthood, one body carrying God's presence into the world, the way Israel was called to be a kingdom of priests in Exodus 19.

Which means you cannot do this alone. Stones do not stack themselves. A stone by itself in a field is not a house, it is a rock.

And Peter is honest about the other outcome. The same stone that becomes a foundation for one person becomes a stone of stumbling for another. One stone, two opposite effects, and the difference is entirely your response to who he is. There is no third option where Jesus is simply irrelevant. You either build your life on him or you trip over him.

But you are not among those who stumble. You came. You are being built.

Reflect

Where has the verdict of a room been mattering more to you than the verdict of God?

Peter says you are being built up, present tense, ongoing. What is God building in you right now that is not finished?

Stones do not stack themselves. Who are the people you are actually being built together with, and are you letting them near enough to do it?

03

The Name Before the Ask

But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession, that you may proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light. Once you were not a people, but now you are God's people; once you had not received mercy, but now you have received mercy.

1 Peter 2:9 to 10 · ESV

This is the center of the chapter, and it is one long sentence of pure gift.

Every title here was first spoken over Israel at Sinai. A chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession. Peter takes the language of Exodus 19 and Isaiah 43 and lays it over a room full of Gentile outsiders in Roman provinces who had never had any claim to it. Paul calls this being grafted in, and he attaches a warning worth keeping: do not be arrogant about it. This was never earned. It was given.

Then read verse 10 slowly, because the order in it will preach to you for the rest of your life.

Once you were not a people, but now you are God's people. Once you had not received mercy, but now you have received mercy.

He does not say become God's people and then mercy will find you. Mercy came first. Mercy is what made you a people at all. You were not assembled and then approved. You were shown mercy, and the mercy is what assembled you.

Now the purpose clause. That you may proclaim the excellencies of him who called you. That word for proclaim appears exactly once in the entire New Testament, right here. Peter reached for a rare word because he wanted a rare thing. It carries the sense of announcing outward, publicly, beyond your own circle.

And it happens two ways at once. Your words announce him and your life announces him, and neither works alone. Declaration with no changed life is the hypocrisy Peter told you to put away on day one. A changed life with no declaration leaves people admiring you instead of seeing him.

Out of darkness into his marvelous light. That is not a graduation you worked toward. It is a rescue you were carried through.

Reflect

Read verse 10 out loud, slowly. Where in your life have you had the order backwards, trying to become God's people in order to receive mercy?

Which of the four names is hardest for you to actually believe about yourself today?

Where is your life proclaiming him well, and where are your words and your life saying two different things?

04

Honorable Under Pressure

Beloved, I urge you as sojourners and exiles to abstain from the passions of the flesh, which wage war against your soul. Keep your conduct among the Gentiles honorable, so that when they speak against you as evildoers, they may see your good deeds and glorify God on the day of visitation.

1 Peter 2:11 to 17 · ESV

Beloved. Peter opens the second half of the chapter with affection, not a warning. Whatever he is about to ask, he is asking of people he loves.

Sojourners and exiles. Resident aliens. People with an address here and a citizenship somewhere else. Not tourists passing through and not natives settling in, but something harder than both, present and invested and never quite belonging.

Abstain from the passions of the flesh, which wage war against your soul. Peter is not being precious about small pleasures. He says there is a war on, and your soul is the ground being fought over. What you feed is not neutral.

Then the strategy, and it is worth reading carefully because it is easy to hear as weakness. Keep your conduct among the Gentiles honorable, so that when they speak against you as evildoers, they may see your good deeds and glorify God.

Notice Peter says when, not if. He assumes you will be spoken against. These believers were being called evildoers for doing good, slandered for the very things that had changed in them.

His answer is not retaliation and it is not retreat. It is a life so plainly good that the accusation cannot stick to it. He says elsewhere in this passage that doing good puts to silence the ignorance of foolish people. That is not surrender. That is a weapon. Your honorable conduct is how the slander gets answered without you ever having to answer it.

And here is the line that has to stay attached, because 1 Peter 2 is one of the most misused chapters in the New Testament. Peter is addressing believers attacked for doing good. He is not telling anyone to obey a command to sin, and he is not telling anyone to stay silent about evil. Submission that would require you to disobey God has left the purpose Peter gives it, which is that God would be glorified. You cannot glorify God by sinning against him.

That distinction protects people. Endure the personal cost of being misjudged for doing right. Refuse any demand to do wrong.

Reflect

Where are you currently being misjudged for something that is actually obedience?

What have you been feeding that is quietly waging war against your soul?

Is there a place where you have confused enduring mistreatment with permitting evil? What would honest courage look like there?

05

Follow the Pattern

For to this you have been called, because Christ also suffered for you, leaving you an example, so that you might follow in his steps. He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree, that we might die to sin and live to righteousness. By his wounds you have been healed. For you were straying like sheep, but have now returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of your souls.

1 Peter 2:18 to 25 · ESV

Peter asks a blunt question in this section. What credit is it if you sin, get punished for it, and endure that? None. That is just consequences. But if you do good, suffer for it, and endure, that is a gracious thing in the sight of God.

Then he gives you the model, and the word he chooses is beautiful. The word behind example is the tracing template a schoolchild was given in the ancient world, letters written faintly on the page so a small hand could trace over them and learn the shape. Peter is not saying admire Christ from a distance. He is saying put your hand on the line he already drew.

Look at what he traced. He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth. When he was reviled, he did not revile in return. When he suffered, he did not threaten. Every instinct you feel when someone wrongs you, he felt and did not follow.

What did he do instead? He continued entrusting himself to him who judges justly. That is the whole secret. He was not passive and he was not swallowing it. He was handing the verdict to a Father he trusted to get it right. You can absorb an injustice without retaliating only if you genuinely believe someone else is keeping the books.

And then Peter turns from example to substitute, because Christ is never only a model. He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree. This is more than a body being beaten. This is the sinless One carrying the full weight of what he had never known, our sin credited to him.

Why? That we might die to sin and live to righteousness.

By his wounds you have been healed. Keep that in the sentence Peter put it in. He is quoting Isaiah 53, and the healing he is naming is the healing of what sin did to you. The disease was sin, the cure was the cross, and it is finished. Past tense. Done.

For you were straying like sheep. That is the honest picture of every one of us, wandering after things that never satisfied, no shepherd, no direction. But have now returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of your souls.

You did not find your way back. You were brought.

So when the squeeze comes this week, Peter's answer was never try harder to produce better juice. It is this. Come to him. Be fed by his word. Know the name he gave you. Let him build you together with his people. And when suffering comes, trace the line the Shepherd already drew.

Reflect

Where do you most need to hand a verdict over to the God who judges justly instead of defending yourself?

What does it change to know that the wounds of Christ dealt with your sin completely, in the past tense?

You did not find your way back to the Shepherd. He brought you. Where do you need to rest in that today?

A Prayer of Identity

Father, thank You that You did not wait for me to become someone before You showed me mercy. Once I was not a people, and now I am Yours. Once I had not received mercy, and now I have. Thank You that Jesus is the Living Stone, rejected by men and chosen and precious in Your sight, and that You are building me together with Your people into a house where Your presence lives. Give me the instinct of a newborn for Your word, and clear out of me whatever kills that appetite. When I am misjudged for doing good, keep me from retaliating and teach me to entrust myself to You, the One who judges justly. Thank You that Christ bore my sins in His body on the tree, that I have died to sin and can live to righteousness, and that by His wounds I have been healed. I was straying, and You brought me home. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Declaration

I am a living stone, built on the Living Stone who was rejected by men and chosen by God.

I am a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for His own possession.

Once I was not a people, but now I am God's people. Once I had not received mercy, but now I have received mercy.

I was called out of darkness into His marvelous light, and my life and my words proclaim His excellencies.

Christ bore my sins in His body on the tree, so I have died to sin and I live to righteousness.

I was straying like a sheep, and I have been returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of my soul.

I walk with victory, not to it.

Now go and live as what you already are. May you crave His word like a newborn craves what feeds him. May you be built together with His people into a house where His presence is at home. May you keep your conduct honorable when you are spoken against, entrusting the verdict to the God who judges justly. And when the pressure comes, may what comes out of you be Christ, because He is what has been filling you all along.

Now may the God of peace who brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, the great shepherd of the sheep, by the blood of the eternal covenant, equip you with everything good that you may do his will, working in us that which is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen. (Hebrews 13:20 to 21, ESV)
